## 1. How to use these playbooks

Each playbook lists what to add, what to remove or play down, the order, what to watch for, what to ask, and a before → after example. Numbers and facts in the examples are placeholders: always ask for the user's real ones. Several situations often apply at once (a veteran changing careers, a new grad with a gap); combine the playbooks; where they differ on titles, section 11 governs military roles and section 3 governs everything else. US federal applications have their own rules in industries.md.

Two rules run through every playbook:

- **Explain, don't hide.** A labelled gap or a grouped set of contracts reads better than a timeline the reader has to reconstruct. Never invent employment, stretch dates or blur them to cover a gap.
- **The user owns disclosure.** Health, disability, caregiving detail, convictions, visa status and identity markers go on the page only if the user chooses, after hearing the trade-off.

## 2. Student or new graduate

- **Add:** projects, internships, part-time jobs, leadership in clubs or teams, relevant coursework only where it fills a skills gap, GPA by the rule in SKILL.md.
- **Remove:** high-school items after the second year of university, objective clichés, a skills list padded with soft skills.
- **Order:** Education → Projects or Experience (whichever is stronger for the target) → Skills. One page.
- **Watch:** don't inflate group projects; say what the user did. Part-time and service jobs count: they show reliability, customers and volume.
- **Post-graduation search** (`convention`). Up to about 9 months of job searching after a degree needs no entry and no label; recruiters read the graduation date. Do not add "Career break: job searching". If the user did anything since graduating (a project, a course, freelance work, volunteering, a part-time job), add it with real dates so the resume shows current activity. If they did nothing, say that is normal, suggest starting one small project now and listing it with its start date only once it exists, and offer the interview-prep skill, if available, for a one-sentence answer ("I've been focused on backend roles and building X").
- **Ask:** "Which project are you proudest of, and what did you build yourself?" "Any job, club role or competition where you were responsible for something?"
- **Before → after:** "Member, Robotics Club" → "Robotics Club, Software Lead (2024–2025): [what they built], [result, e.g. placed Nth of M teams]."

## 3. Career changer

- **Add:** a hybrid summary naming the target function; transferable results written in the new field's language; new-field projects, courses and certifications.
- **Remove or play down:** old-field jargon and technical depth the new reader can't use.
- **Order:** Summary → Relevant projects (when strong) → Experience → Education.
- **Watch:** never retitle old jobs with the new field's titles; titles must match what an employer would confirm. Translate the bullets, not the titles.
- **Ask:** "Which parts of your current job look most like the new one?" "What have you built or completed in the new field, and can you link it?"
- **Before → after:** headline "Aspiring Data Analyst" → "Operations analyst moving into data analytics (SQL, Tableau)"; bullet "Prepared weekly store reports" → "Built a weekly SQL report on [n] stores' sales that [what changed]."

## 4. Employment gap (caregiving, health, sabbatical, relocation, study)

- **Add:** a dated entry with a short honest label, such as "Career break: family caregiving · 03/2019–06/2021", plus anything done in the period: courses, freelance work, volunteering, a move.
- **Remove:** medical detail (never required; pre-offer health questions are restricted in the UK and US, `official`). Apologetic wording.
- **Order:** keep month/year dates consistent so the label sits in sequence.
- **Watch:**
  - In a US field experiment, parents returning from a family "opt-out" got about 5% callbacks, against about 15% for continuously employed parents and about 9% for unemployed parents; mothers and fathers were penalised about equally in callbacks, and fathers were rated worse on some measures in a follow-up survey (`study`, Weisshaar 2018). Naming the gap honestly is the default; give this evidence and let the user choose the label's wording.
  - A current gap weighs more than an old one: callbacks fall steeply over the first ~8 months of unemployment and then level off (`study`, Kroft et al.).
  - Germany expects a gapless timeline with labelled gaps.
  - Years-only dates to blur short gaps conflict with US federal rules and background checks; prefer month/year plus a label.
- **Ask:** "What did that period involve, roughly? A short label is enough, like 'Family caregiving' or 'Health recovery'; no details needed." "Did you study, volunteer, freelance or build anything then?"
- **Before → after:** 2019–2021 left blank → "Career break, family caregiving · 03/2019–06/2021" with the bullet "Completed [certificate] (2021); volunteer treasurer, [organisation] ([budget, if the user gives it])".

## 5. Layoff

- **Add:** nothing is required. Optionally one line under the role: "Role eliminated in company-wide restructuring (about 30% reduction)", with the figure only if the user knows it.
- **Remove:** the word "fired", reasons for leaving (`official`, Job Bank).
- **Order:** normal.
- **Watch:** callbacks fall steeply over the first ~8 months of unemployment and then level off; the drop is larger in tight labour markets (`study`, Kroft et al.). Applying soon matters more than how the layoff is worded. A layoff needs no apology on the page.
- **Ask:** "Was it a role elimination or a wider layoff? Do you want that stated, or left for the interview?"

## 6. Freelance, contract, consulting, gig

- **Add:** one umbrella entry ("Independent consultant, 2021–2024") with clients as roles or bullets. Agency contracts as "Contractor via [Agency] → [Client]". "Contract" in the title so short tenures read as planned.
- **Remove:** ten one-line gigs; group them.
- **Order:** normal, dated.
- **Watch:** respect client NDAs ("a Fortune 500 retailer" is fine). Revenue or client counts only from the user.
- **Ask:** "Which clients or projects best match the target role?" "Can you name them, or should we describe them?"
- **Before → after:** five entries of 2–4 months each → one "Freelance front-end developer · 2022–2024" entry with roles for the three most relevant clients.

## 7. Founder

- **Add:** "Founder & CEO" (or the honest title), scale (team, revenue, users, funding) and an honest outcome: acquired, wound down, ongoing.
- **Remove:** vanity titles for a one-person side project, unless stated honestly ("Founder, solo project").
- **Watch:** founders applying for employee roles should stress shipping, collaboration and working within constraints.
- **Ask:** "How many people, users or customers at its peak? What happened to it?"

## 8. Many short stints

- **Add:** grouped contracts; one entry with roles for internal moves; a one-line reason only when structural (acquisition, closure, funding ended).
- **Remove:** sub-3-month roles that add nothing, if the user wants. Leaving them off a resume is acceptable; leaving them off a federal or background-check form that asks for full history is not.
- **Ask:** "Were any of these contracts or temp roles by design?" "Did any end because of a closure or acquisition?"

## 9. Older worker

- **Add:** the last 10–15 years in detail; "Earlier career: roles at X and Y" without dates.
- **Remove (optional):** graduation years more than 15–20 years old, obsolete technology, "30+ years of experience".
- **Order:** normal; 2 pages.
- **Watch:** field experiments find hiring discrimination against older applicants, strongest for older women (`study`, Neumark et al.). A missing graduation date can itself signal age, so it's the user's call. US federal applications still need dates.
- **Ask:** "Which older roles would a hiring manager for this job still care about?"

## 10. Senior or executive

- **Add:** scope for every role (P&L or budget, headcount, geography), the mandate (turnaround, IPO, integration) and the outcome; board and committee seats with dates.
- **Remove:** individual-contributor detail from more than 10 years ago.
- **Order:** Leadership summary → Experience → Governance → Education. 2–3 pages.
- **Watch:** board searches often want a separate one-page board biography as well (`convention`, not verified this pass).
- **Ask:** "For each role: budget or P&L size, headcount, and what changed because of you?"

## 11. Military to civilian

- **Add:** civilian titles and vocabulary, mapped with the O\*NET Military Crosswalk or My Next Move for Veterans; scope in people led, budget and equipment value; a clearance line for defence roles (level, agency, status).
- **Remove:** acronyms, unit names and occupation codes in the body; awards only the military recognises (keep major ones, translated into what they recognised); combat specifics (`convention`, university veterans' guidance).
- **Order:** hybrid summary → Experience → Education and training → Certifications.
- **Watch:** never disclose classified detail. Scale numbers must come from the user. Common translations: Squad Leader → Team Leader; Commander → Director (`convention`). Pick the civilian title the crosswalk and the user's actual duties support, and keep the military title in parentheses so it can be verified.
- **Ask:** "What was your occupation code and final rank?" "How many people, how much equipment or budget were you responsible for?" "Any clearance, and is it active?" "Which civilian role are you aiming for?"
- **Before → after:** "PLT SGT, 2/75 RGR; NCOIC S4; AAM x2" → "Operations and Logistics Manager (Platoon Sergeant), US Army · Led [n] personnel; accountable for $[x] of equipment; [measured result]."
- **Titles when a veteran changes field** (`convention`). Translate the function, not the target. Use the civilian title that describes what the user actually did (Operations Manager, Logistics Manager, Team Leader), with the official rank or billet in parentheses. Use "Project Manager" only if the role was mainly running defined projects with scope, schedule and budget, and the user can name them. Otherwise keep the functional title and write the bullets in project-management language (scope, timeline, budget, stakeholders, delivered on date). This is consistent with the career-changer rule in section 3: the parenthetical keeps the title verifiable.
- **Into project management** (`convention`): lead with delivery evidence (scope, budget, timeline, people, what was delivered and when), name methods only if the posting does, and list a project-management certification only if the user holds it. See industries.md, section 4a.
- **Outside the US** (`convention`, not verified this pass): leave out clearance lines unless the employer is a defence contractor for that same government. Translation sources: the UK Career Transition Partnership; Australia's veteransemployment.gov.au. In the Gulf and elsewhere, state the service, final rank and years, translated into civilian scope. Nationality and visa lines follow countries.md (the user's choice). Ask: "Which country's forces, and is the job in that country?"

## 12. Academic CV

- **Add:** the complete record: appointments, publications in the discipline's style, grants (name, agency, dates, amount, purpose), presentations, teaching, mentoring, service, honours, memberships, references.
- **Remove:** nothing that belongs to the record; completeness is expected.
- **Order:** Education first. Put publications before teaching when research is the selling point and later when teaching is (`convention`, university writing centres).
- **Watch:** keep it a separate document from any industry resume. "In preparation" never sits in the published list.

## 13. PhD to industry

- **Add:** a 1–2 page resume; a summary aimed at the role; the PhD written as a job ("Doctoral Researcher, University X"); research projects written as projects with impact; "Selected publications" (2–5).
- **Remove:** the full publication list, teaching evaluations, conference minutiae.
- **Order:** Summary → Experience (including the PhD) → Skills → Education.
- **Watch:** translate academic work into industry terms: grant writing → securing funding; supervising students → leadership; methods → tools and outcomes (`convention`, university careers guidance).
- **Before → after:** a 9-page CV with 31 papers → 2 pages, "Doctoral Researcher" as a job, "Selected publications (3 of 31)".

## 14. Returner and returnships

- **Add:** a career-break entry; refreshed skills and recent certifications; volunteer, school or community leadership written like work.
- **Remove:** apologetic language.
- **Order:** hybrid.
- **Watch:** returnship programmes exist for this group (`convention`, not verified this pass); point the user to search for them in their field and country.

## 15. International student or visa holder

- **Add:** a work-authorisation line only when it removes a false impression, such as "Authorized to work in the US (STEM OPT through 06/2028)", or citizenship or permanent residence when a foreign degree might suggest otherwise.
- **Remove:** visa type when it doesn't help, nationality, passport numbers.
- **Watch:** visa status need not be on a US resume, but never lie when asked (university career centres). Defence roles may require citizenship to be stated. Outside the US, see countries.md for how each market expects work rights (Australia and the Gulf expect them; the UK accepts a right-to-work line).
- **Ask:** "Will you need sponsorship now or later? Do you want that on the page, or answered in the application form?"

## 16. Disability

- **Default:** not on the resume. Disclosing a disability in a field experiment cut employer interest by about 26%, concentrated among experienced applicants and small firms not covered by the ADA (`study`, Ameri et al.).
- **Exceptions:** when the user wants it, for example roles in the disability sector, or eligibility for a specific hiring programme. US federal Schedule A eligibility is optional, with the letter submitted separately (`official`, OPM).
- **Watch:** this is entirely the user's decision. Give the evidence once, then format what they choose.

## 17. Criminal record

- **Add:** work, training and credentials gained during incarceration, credited to the employer entity (for example state correctional industries) or the awarding college rather than the prison.
- **Remove:** conviction details; the resume is not the place.
- **Order:** hybrid; explain the gap in a cover letter or interview if the user chooses.
- **Watch:** in the UK, most spent convictions need not be disclosed for most jobs under the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 as amended in October 2023 (`official`, verify for the specific role). Answer application forms truthfully and only what is asked. Some licensing boards bar certain professions. Ban-the-box laws hide records early, but a field experiment found they widened racial callback gaps, consistent with employers guessing (`study`); that is context, not advice to disclose.

## 18. Chosen name

- Use the chosen name on the resume and email. The legal name is typically needed only for background checks and right-to-work paperwork after an offer (`convention`, not verified).
- Never print a deadname. Never change any identity signal unless the user asks.
- Name-based discrimination is measured (`study`: Black-coded names got 2.1 percentage points fewer contacts, concentrated in a minority of firms; "whitened" resumes got more than twice the callbacks, even at employers advertising diversity). If the user asks about anglicising or shortening a name, give this evidence and let them decide; never suggest it unprompted.

## 19. Intake question bank

Ask only what the resume, the conversation or the user's Career data (MCP) doesn't already show: at most two questions at a time by default, or a numbered batch of up to five when the user wants speed.

- "Which country, or countries, are you applying in? Any particular employer type: private, government, academic?"
- "Paste one posting you're excited about." It decides what counts as relevant.
- "Anything in the last two years I should know about: a break, a layoff, contract work, a move?"
- "For the gap: what did that period involve, roughly? A short honest label is enough."
- "Do you want pronouns, a photo or your visa status on the page? I'll tell you the trade-offs and you decide."
- "Which of these older roles would a hiring manager for this job still care about?"
- "What is your GPA, on what scale, and did the posting ask for it?"
- "Were any of these short roles contracts by design?"
