## 1. Payments per year

Before annualising any salary, ask: **"How many salary payments a year, and which are guaranteed by law, by a collective agreement or by the contract?"** Then annual base = payment × number of payments. A 13th or 14th payment that is customary but not contractual is `conditional`.

| Country     | What changes the comparison                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spain       | At least 2 extra payments a year (14, or prorated into 12), Estatuto de los Trabajadores art. 31                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Austria     | Usually quoted as 14 payments under collective agreements (KV); the 13th and 14th are taxed at 6% after a €620 allowance, within 1/6 of annual pay and up to about €25,000 (higher bands pay more; verify with a payroll adviser). Sources disagree on whether they're statutory |
| Germany     | No statutory 13th salary or Weihnachtsgeld; it comes from a collective agreement, the contract or established company practice (§3)                                                                                                                                              |
| Mexico      | Aguinaldo of at least 15 days' pay, due by 20 Dec (LFT art. 87)                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Brazil      | 13º salário (Lei 4.090/1962) in two instalments, the second by 20 Dec; plus a one-third vacation bonus and 8% FGTS paid by the employer                                                                                                                                          |

## 2. India

**Decode CTC.** Cost to company is not take-home and never comparable to a US base. Ask for the payslip-level breakup and split it into:

1. Fixed cash (basic, DA, allowances).
2. Variable: `conditional`; ask for the actual payout % in the last 2 years.
3. Employer PF: 12% of basic, often calculated on the ₹15,000 wage ceiling (re-notified 29 May 2026).
4. Gratuity: often provisioned at about 4.81% of basic but paid only on leaving after 5 years of service (fixed-term employees: after 1 year), at 15/26 × last basic+DA × years, capped at ₹20 lakh. Don't count it as yearly cash.
5. Insurance, joining bonus (with its clawback), relocation, ESOPs, retention bonuses: listed separately; one-offs never count as recurring.

**Labour Codes, in force since 21 Nov 2025.** Wages (basic + DA + retaining allowance) must be at least 50% of total remuneration; excluded allowances above that count as wages. That raises the PF and gratuity base and can lower take-home pay at the same CTC, so ask for the revised structure. Central rules followed around May 2026; many state rules are still pending. Sources differ on whether the 50% test runs against total remuneration or CTC, and whether gratuity under the new wage definition applies retrospectively to the whole service is disputed. Vendor estimates of the take-home effect ("3–5% lower") are not data. Many online CTC calculators still use pre-Code assumptions.

**Current CTC questions** ("current CTC, expected CTC, notice period") are routine and lawful, and often checked against payslips. The user decides whether to answer; if they do, they give the true total with its breakup, never inflated, and frame the expected figure around the role and market data rather than a percentage on current pay. "30–50% hike on switching" comes from vendor and candidate surveys, not market truth.

**Notice and buyout.** Contracts typically set about 15–30 days at startups and 60–90 days at large firms and IT services. Buyout terms come from the contract, not statute: check whether the user can buy out unilaterally or needs consent. Ask the new employer, in writing, whether it reimburses the buyout or sizes a joining bonus to cover it, and whether an earlier joining date is wanted.

**Non-competes.** Post-employment non-competes are generally void under Contract Act s.27 (_Superintendence Co. v. Krishan Murgai_, 1980); restraints during employment and notice are enforceable, and non-solicitation clauses are often upheld.

**Reneging.** Offer-shopping during long notice periods is common, and employers renege too (IITs have barred firms that revoked campus offers). Advise against accepting two offers; if the user must renege, do it early and honestly, and check the joining-bonus clawback.

**Public sector:** the pay-matrix cell is set (decoding.md §8).

Worked example: 32 LPA CTC, 20% variable, employer PF and gratuity included, a 2 LPA joining bonus with a 12-month clawback, current notice 90 days.

- Variable 6.4 LPA is `conditional`: ask for the last 2 years' payout %.
- Ask whether the 2 LPA joining bonus sits inside the 32 LPA. It's a one-off either way.
- Fixed cash is under 25.6 LPA (32 − 6.4) once PF, gratuity, insurance and any bonus inside CTC come out. The exact figure is `unknown` until the breakup arrives; ask for it under the post-Code structure.
- Asks: more fixed and less variable; buyout reimbursement in writing; a joining date that fits the 90 days or the buyout. Never misstate current CTC.

## 3. Germany and Austria

- **Pay structure:** salaries are discussed as gross annual pay (Bruttojahresgehalt). Ask whether it's paid in 12 or 13 instalments and whether any Weihnachtsgeld or bonus is contractual. In one survey, 77% of employees covered by a collective agreement got Christmas pay against 41% of those not covered.
- **Expectations question:** in an application or a first call, give one gross annual figure and say whether it assumes 12 or 13 payments. German guides often prefer a single figure to a range. Public occupational median pay: the Bundesagentur für Arbeit's Entgeltatlas.
- **Notice (§622 BGB):** the base period is 4 weeks to the 15th or the end of a month; 2 weeks during probation. The employee's notice can't be longer than the employer's. Contracts and collective agreements often set more.
- **Post-contract non-competes:** check for one and whether it pays compensation during the restraint period; German law generally requires compensation for it to bind (verify with a Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht).
- **Startup equity:** often a virtual (phantom) option plan rather than real shares; see decoding.md §3.
- **Austria:** see §1 for 14 payments; collective agreements (KV) set minimum pay by grade, and a quoted figure may be monthly.
- **Tarif or AT:** ask whether the role is tariff-bound (Tarif) or außertariflich (AT). Tariff roles: negotiate the pay group (Entgeltgruppe) and step (Stufe) with evidence. TVöD/TV-L public roles: ask for credit for relevant prior experience when the step is set (verify).
- **EU directive:** Germany had missed the deadline with no adopted bill as of July 2026 (§4).

## 4. Rest of the EU

**Pay Transparency Directive 2023/970, Article 5.** Applicants are entitled to the initial pay or its range (set by objective, gender-neutral criteria) in the vacancy notice, before the interview or otherwise in time for an informed negotiation, plus, where applicable, the relevant collective-agreement terms. Employers may not ask about pay history. They may still ask about expectations, but asking before giving the range may conflict with Article 5 (Freshfields).

**Transposition (as of mid-2026):** the deadline was 7 June 2026 and the Commission refused to postpone it. Italy, Lithuania, Malta and Slovakia were reported complete by the deadline (trackers disagree on Malta); Greece enacted in July 2026 with main duties from 1 Nov 2026. Sweden's government decided in March 2026 not to submit a bill and to seek postponement and renegotiation. Germany missed the deadline; its cabinet discussion slipped to August 2026 (check whether a draft has since been adopted). Spain had no draft as of June. The Netherlands targets 1 Jan 2027; France expects a parliamentary vote in spring 2027. Poland already applies the applicant rules (pay information before the interview, no pay-history question) from 24 Dec 2025. Directives generally don't bind private employers directly, so where the country hasn't transposed, treat the range request as reasonable rather than enforceable. Practitioners disagree; verify locally.

## 5. United Kingdom

- No pay-range posting law and no salary-history ban. A government equal-pay consultation (14 July–27 Oct 2026) proposes pay information in adverts or in writing before interview; "the UK is banning salary-history questions" is not in the official summary.
- **Notice:** the employee owes at least 1 week after 1 month's service; the employer owes at least 1 week after 1 month's service, then 1 week per complete year of service from 2 years, up to 12 weeks. Contracts usually set more.
- **Accepting can bind.** An accepted offer can already be a contract before the start date: in _Kankanalapalli v Loesche_ [2026] EAT 49 the tribunal applied 3 months' notice to an offer the employer withdrew. Pulling out after acceptance may also breach the contract; read it.
- **Non-competes:** a government working paper (26 Nov 2025) floated a 3-month cap, a ban, or a ban below a salary threshold; it closed on 18 Feb 2026 and the law is unchanged so far. About 5 million GB employees have non-competes, typically around 6 months.
- **Civil Service and NHS:** decoding.md §8. Pension: decoding.md §6.

## 6. United States

- **At-will employment:** an offer can usually be withdrawn lawfully, before or after a counter. Courts split on reliance claims after a candidate resigns (Nebraska's _Goff-Hamel_ allowed reliance damages; New York rejects such claims). So the user resigns only once the final offer is signed and contingencies have cleared.
- **Clawbacks:** California and New York limits in decoding.md §5.
- **Visa-dependent users:** pay level affects H-1B. DHS weighted, wage-level-based cap selection took effect on 27 Feb 2026; a DOL proposal (27 Mar 2026) would raise prevailing-wage levels and wasn't final as of the latest sources. Flag it and refer to an immigration lawyer; don't advise.
- **Federal jobs:** decoding.md §8.

## 7. Canada

- **British Columbia:** pay-history questions banned since May 2023; ranges required in public postings since 1 Nov 2023, and open-ended ranges or "competitive" don't count.
- **Ontario** (from 1 Jan 2026, employers with 25 or more employees): postings state the expected pay or a range no wider than $50,000 (roles over $200,000 exempt), disclose AI used in screening and whether a vacancy exists, and employers must tell interviewed applicants within 45 days of the last interview whether a hiring decision has been made.
- Other provinces: check locally.

## 8. Gulf states

- **UAE end-of-service gratuity (onshore):** payable after at least 1 year of service; 21 days of **basic** pay per year for the first 5 years, 30 days per year after that, capped at 2 years' basic. Housing and transport allowances are excluded, so an allowance-heavy package lowers it. Sources conflict on calendar vs working days and on the cap's basis; verify. Free zones differ: DIFC uses DEWS contributions instead of gratuity.
- **Saudi Arabia:** half a month's last wage per year for the first 5 years, a full month after. Sources disagree on whether allowances count (most legal sources read "last wage" as including housing and regular allowances; some calculators use basic); verify.
- **Levers:** the basic vs allowance split, housing, annual flights, schooling, notice.
- Check the government-registered (MOHRE) contract against the offer letter before signing; they should match.

## 9. Pay-transparency and salary-history law snapshot (2026)

| Jurisdiction | Pay range in postings           | Pay-history question                  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| US           | Required in some states (below) | Banned in some states (below)         |
| EU           | Article 5 where transposed (§4) | Banned where transposed               |
| UK           | None (consultation open, §5)    | Not banned                            |
| BC, Ontario  | Required (§7)                   | BC: banned. Ontario: no ban confirmed |
| India        | Not required                    | Routine and lawful (§2)               |

**US detail.**

- **Posting laws:** about 13–18 states plus DC, depending on the tracker. Recent: NJ (1 Jun 2025), VT (1 Jul 2025), MA (29 Oct 2025, employers with 25+; the range excludes bonus, equity and benefits), CA's good-faith "pay on hire" scale (1 Jan 2026), VA (1 Jul 2026, all employer sizes), ME (29 Jul 2026, employers with 10+ employees; postings by third parties included), CT proactive disclosure (1 Oct 2026), DE (26 Sep 2027).
- **Salary-history bans:** roughly 17–22 states plus localities, depending on the tracker (HR Dive, April 2026: 22 state-wide, 24 local), including Virginia from 1 Jul 2026, where pay history the candidate volunteers may be used only to justify a higher offer. Expectation questions stay legal, and most bans let candidates volunteer their history.

**What the posted range means.** In Colorado, guidance lets employers pay outside a good-faith range, so its top isn't always a cap. Glassdoor's own study found posted ranges contained reported pay only about 67% of the time, with pay skewed to the lower half: ask where in the range the offer sits and why. **Effects:** Colorado's posting law raised wages 1.3–3.6% without widening ranges (NBER w34480); coworker ("horizontal") transparency can lower average pay about 2% because employers bargain harder to avoid renegotiating with everyone, so expect "everyone at this level is paid the same"; salary-history bans raised private-sector job-changers' pay about 3.9%, with larger gains for women (about 6.2%) and non-white workers (about 5.8%) (Bessen, Denk & Meng, via BU TPRI 2024).

## 10. Notice periods and non-competes

- **US:** the FTC's non-compete rule was vacated and the FTC withdrew its appeals on 5 Sep 2025; state law governs.
  - Near-total bans: CA, MN, ND, OK.
  - Wyoming restricts contracts from 1 Jul 2025, with executive and trade-secret carve-outs.
  - Washington voids nearly all non-competes, existing ones included, from 30 Jun 2027; until then they apply only above a 2026 earnings threshold of $126,858.83.
  - Florida's CHOICE Act (July 2025) lets employers of high earners (base at least twice the county mean wage) enforce up to 4 years of non-compete or garden-leave notice, with mandatory injunctions. Read those clauses closely.
  - Tennessee: no non-competes under $70,000 pay from July 2026. Virginia (SB 170): unenforceable after termination without cause unless severance was paid and disclosed.
- **UK:** §5. **India:** §2. **Germany:** §3.

Legitimate asks: a narrower scope or shorter duration, paid garden leave, a non-solicit instead of a non-compete, and a start date that respects the current notice. A broad or unpaid non-compete goes to an employment lawyer before signing.
