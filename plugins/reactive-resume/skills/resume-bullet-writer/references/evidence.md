# Evidence behind this skill

## 1. What the research supports

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Strength                                                          | Rule it supports                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Better resume writing helps. In a field experiment with about 480,000 jobseekers, non-generative writing help (spelling, grammar, clarity) raised hires by about 8%, with no drop in employer satisfaction.                                                                           | Strong (randomised, published in Management Science)              | Rewriting the user's own facts clearly is worth doing.                                                                      |
| AI-polished applications lost signal value. On freelance platforms, after AI writing tools arrived, how well an application matched the job stopped predicting hiring as strongly, employers leaned more on work history, and time spent editing AI drafts went with better outcomes. | Moderate (two 2025 platform studies; one uses a structural model) | Lead with checkable specifics; offer variants and invite the user to edit.                                                  |
| Small errors cost interviews. In an experiment with 445 recruiters, a few spelling errors cut interview probability by about 7 percentage points and many errors by about 18.                                                                                                         | Moderate (scenario experiment)                                    | Lint for typos and spell names the user's way.                                                                              |
| Moderate self-promotion helps; maximal self-promotion hurts. Candidates with the most intense self-promotion were rated lower and seen as manipulative.                                                                                                                               | Moderate (one study)                                              | Plain verbs, no hype.                                                                                                       |
| People under-claim, unequally. For the same performance, women described their own performance less favourably than men, including when the description went to a potential employer and when incentives to self-promote were removed.                                                | Strong (large experiments, QJE 2022)                              | Probe for under-claiming; ask "what did you do?" (Third-person prompts are a practitioner technique the study didn't test.) |
| Specific, baselined results are what recruiters and career centres ask for. A former Google HR head's formula adds a baseline for comparison; university career centres teach the same verb + action + result skeleton.                                                               | Practitioner consensus                                            | XYZ with a baseline as the default; CAR and scope as alternatives.                                                          |
| First reads are fast and visual. Clear headings and bulleted accomplishments did well in eye-tracking work; long sentences and clutter did poorly.                                                                                                                                    | Weak (vendor study, sample undisclosed)                           | Short bullets, strongest information first.                                                                                 |
| Hiring managers say they are wary of AI-written resumes but identify them poorly: about a third picked the AI-written resume correctly in a four-resume test, and about half accept AI for drafting or proofreading.                                                                  | Weak (vendor survey, US, 2025)                                    | Specific, human-sounding text costs nothing; fabrication is the real risk.                                                  |
| Misstatements have consequences. A recruiter at a large search firm estimated that 5–10% of offers are rescinded over resume misstatements.                                                                                                                                           | Weak (one expert estimate)                                        | Never invent; run the defensibility test.                                                                                   |

## 2. Myths and where they came from

| Claim                                                        | Origin and problem                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recruiters spend 6 (or 7.4) seconds on a resume.             | Ladders eye-tracking studies (2012, n=30; 2018, sample undisclosed). A vendor that sells rewrites; measures a first triage glance. Another study found a 31-second median. |
| 75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before a human sees them. | A 2012 sales pitch from Preptel, which closed in 2013. No study or method was ever published.                                                                              |
| Quantified resumes get 40% more interviews (or 2.5× more).   | Attributed to TalentWorks, "HBR" or a "LinkedIn 2023 report"; none could be traced, and citations disagree on the numbers.                                                 |
| Only 1 in 10 resumes includes measurable results.            | Vendor claim with no method; conflicts with another vendor's data.                                                                                                         |
| Every bullet needs a number.                                 | No evidence. MIT and Missouri State career guides say otherwise, and the rule pushes people to invent numbers.                                                             |
| XYZ is Google's official format.                             | Laszlo Bock's personal 2014 LinkedIn post. Useful, not a Google requirement; most copies drop his baseline step.                                                           |
| Hiring managers spot AI resumes in 20 seconds.               | Misreads a 2025 TopResume survey: 20 seconds was the average among the third who guessed correctly.                                                                        |
| "Spearheaded" means ChatGPT wrote it.                        | A recruiter's anecdote citing search trends, not resume text. No study of real submissions exists.                                                                         |
| "Delve" is the tell-tale AI word.                            | Dated: its use fell sharply in 2025, and tell words shift with each model generation. Detection of AI text by people or tools is unreliable.                               |
| AI writing help always hurts candidates.                     | Contradicted by the 480,000-jobseeker experiment above. The platform studies show reduced signal, not a blanket penalty.                                                   |
| 85% of applicants lie on resumes.                            | The HireRight figure is the share of employers who found at least one misstatement, from a screening vendor.                                                               |
| If unsure, round your guess down.                            | Prior-art advice. It still produces an invented number; ask instead.                                                                                                       |
| Two-page resumes are 2.3× preferred.                         | A vendor simulation (ResumeGo, 2018), not real hiring.                                                                                                                     |
| Federal resumes should be long and detailed.                 | Outdated as of 2026: OPM capped Title 5 resumes at 2 pages from 27 September 2025.                                                                                         |
| Write for AI screeners because they favour AI text.          | Overreads a simulation study whose headline numbers changed between versions.                                                                                              |

## 3. Contested or regional points

- **How much to quantify.** Career centres say "where possible"; some say it isn't the central focus. Default: about half to two-thirds of bullets for most roles, fewer for creative, academic and care roles.
- **Tense of completed wins in the current role.** Sources split. Pick one and keep it consistent.
- **Mirroring the job ad's verbs.** Some career centres recommend it for screening software; post-AI evidence says alignment alone carries less signal. Mirror real skills, not phrasing.
- **Strong-verb lists.** Still taught, now read as AI-like when stacked. Treat them as contested.
- **Ranges on the page.** Prefer an honest lower bound ("over 15%") to a range, except for natural frequency ranges ("40–50 a week").
- **Team metrics on an individual resume.** Acceptable with scope wording.
- **Regional norms** (UK, Japan, Germany, US federal): see situations.md; Germany is practitioner consensus only.
