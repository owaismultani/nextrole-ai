## 1. What the research supports

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Rule in this skill                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| After correcting an earlier overcorrection for range restriction, structured interviews have the highest mean validity of common selection methods, about .42 (80% credibility interval .18–.66). Unstructured interviews fall to about .19 (Sackett et al., 2022, 2023).                                  | Run a planned, anchored interview, not free chat.                           |
| Past-behaviour questions with descriptively anchored rating scales averaged validity .63, against .47 for situational questions with anchors (Taylor & Small, 2002).                                                                                                                                       | Default to "Tell me about a time…"; score against written anchors.          |
| Structure includes the same questions for everyone, job analysis, anchored scales, limited and planned follow-up, and note-taking (Campion et al., 1997; Levashina et al., 2014).                                                                                                                          | Plan probes per question; record unplanned help as a hint.                  |
| OPM's format: a lead question plus standard probes (who was involved, what you did, the outcome), rated against proficiency benchmarks; panel members rate alone, then resolve discrepancies.                                                                                                              | Lead question, probes and anchors per competency; independent panel scores. |
| Coaching on the format and on skills raised structured-interview ratings in an experiment (N = 144), partly through knowing what the format rewards (Tross & Maurer, 2008).                                                                                                                                | Explain the rubric outside the role and run repetitions.                    |
| Interview anxiety correlates about r = −.19 with interview performance (Powell et al., 2018); virtual interview training improved performance and reduced anxiety (Langer et al., 2016).                                                                                                                   | Warm-up and coach mode first; Hard is opt-in.                               |
| A small randomised trial of virtual-reality interview practice in adults with autism (n = 26: 16 trained, 10 controls) improved live role-play scores (p = .046), using friendly, business-like and brusque tiers and gated progression (Smith et al., 2014). Evidence for general job-seekers is thinner. | Tiered difficulty; step up after strong answers.                            |
| Feedback helps on average, but a large minority of feedback interventions made performance worse, especially feedback aimed at the self (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996). Task and process feedback beats praise (Hattie & Timperley, 2007).                                                                        | One task-focused fix; no personality verdicts.                              |
| Retrieval practice beats rereading on delayed tests (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006).                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Retry from memory after feedback.                                           |
| Deliberate practice explains under 1% of performance variance in professions (Macnamara et al., 2014).                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Don't oversell drilling; target the weakest dimension.                      |
| Follow-up questioning increased interview faking; past-behaviour questions resisted faking better than situational ones (Levashina & Campion, 2007).                                                                                                                                                       | Never argue honesty from detection; coach truthful stories.                 |
| Interviewers are poor at detecting deceptive impression management, yet what they perceive shifts their ratings (Roulin et al., 2015).                                                                                                                                                                     | Score substance, not polish; never coach fabrication.                       |
| Single interviews are volatile: on interviewing.io only about 25% of repeat interviewees performed consistently (platform data, 2016).                                                                                                                                                                     | Never present one session as a verdict; trends need three or more sessions. |
| LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases; writing evidence before the rating reduces bias (Wang et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023). An open evaluator model reached GPT-4-level agreement with human raters when given a score rubric and a reference answer (Kim et al., 2023).     | Quote first, then score against anchors and the calibration pair.           |
| Assistants give more positive feedback when the user says they wrote the text, and often abandon correct answers when challenged (Sharma et al., 2023).                                                                                                                                                    | The anti-sycophancy rules in SKILL.md.                                      |
| Persona and instruction drift appeared within about eight dialogue rounds in 2024 models (Li et al., 2024); newer models may drift less.                                                                                                                                                                   | Re-anchor every 6–8 turns.                                                  |

## 2. Myths and dubious statistics

Correct these when the user repeats them, and don't cite them yourself.

| Claim                                                                   | What's actually known                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Structured interviews have .51 validity" (Schmidt & Hunter, 1998)      | Outdated. Revised to about .42; still the top-ranked method.                                                                          |
| "Unstructured interviews are a coin flip"                               | Overstated. About .19: weaker, but above zero.                                                                                        |
| "Interviewers decide in 7 seconds / 90 seconds / 4 minutes"             | No traceable sound study. Research suggests fast decisions are overstated, and consistent questions slow them (Frieder et al., 2016). |
| "55% body language, 38% tone, 7% words"                                 | Mehrabian's equations apply only to communicating feelings or attitudes with inconsistent cues, not to interview content.             |
| "STAR is research-validated"                                            | A practitioner convention. The research validates the interviewer's side: past-behaviour questions with anchored scoring.             |
| "Spend 20/10/60/10% on S/T/A/R"                                         | A useful MIT career-office guide, not a measured norm.                                                                                |
| "Always answer in under two minutes"                                    | No research norm; Amazon advises concise answers and offering more detail on request.                                                 |
| "Never say 'we'"                                                        | Overcorrection. Amazon asks for "I" for your own part; UK Civil Service indicators reward crediting colleagues.                       |
| "Google found four interviews predict with 86% confidence"              | Google-internal analysis; the original page is gone. Unverifiable.                                                                    |
| "Bar Raisers have veto power"                                           | Widely repeated; Amazon's public page only describes them as interviewers from another team focused on hiring quality.                |
| "This tool raises your offer rate by X%"                                | Vendor marketing; controlled evidence is limited to small trials.                                                                     |
| "Your score in one mock reflects your true level"                       | Single-session scores are volatile.                                                                                                   |
| "Candidates know when they bombed"                                      | Self-ratings explained only about a quarter of the variance in interviewer ratings in one platform's data.                            |
| "Reframing anxiety as excitement reliably boosts interview performance" | Small lab tasks only (Brooks, 2014). Optional, low-cost, unproven for interviews.                                                     |
| "People speak at exactly 150 words a minute"                            | A rough conversational figure from secondary sources; use only to convert lengths.                                                    |

## 3. Regional and contested advice

- **"I" versus "we".** US career centres push "I". Practitioners report that modest, team-first framing is valued more in some cultures (not verified here). Keep the ownership probe, but soften the tone advice by region.
- **UK strengths questions.** No right or wrong answers, and candidates are told not to rehearse; don't apply STAR.
- **Hypotheticals.** Google uses them deliberately for problem solving; Amazon is behaviour-first. Mix question types by target company.
- **Stress interviews.** Rare in structured hiring and contested; OPM recommends non-threatening settings.
- **Salary questions.** Salary-history bans and pay-transparency rules vary by US state, city and country. Give no legal advice.
- **Unlawful questions.** The EEOC guidance used here is US-specific; other jurisdictions weren't researched.
- **AI during interviews.** Employer-specific and changing fast. Google and Anthropic forbid AI help in live interviews; some employers run declared AI-assisted rounds. Ask about the target employer's rule.
- **Case formats.** Interviewer-led versus candidate-led differs by firm; check the firm's own practice cases.
- **Video and appearance.** US guidance discourages requesting photos before hire. Give no appearance feedback unless asked.

## 4. Changes in 2025–2026

- Google reintroduced in-person interviews for some roles, states AI tools aren't permitted in interviews, and refreshed its structured-interviewing guide in March 2026 (role-related knowledge, problem solving, leadership).
- Amazon uses consent-based AI transcription in some video interviews.
- Anthropic published candidate AI guidance in July 2025: AI is encouraged for preparation, not for live interviews or inventing experience.
- Canva has expected AI tools in its technical interviews since June 2025; Meta reportedly began an AI-enabled coding round in October 2025.
- Hello Interview ended live one-to-one mocks on 31 May 2026; Exponent rebranded as Aced and Pramp sessions moved to Aced Practice.
- Some models drop earlier reasoning between turns, so a hidden scorecard can't rely on private reasoning persisting; the transcript is the source of truth.

## 5. Lessons from earlier practice tools

- Google Interview Warmup deliberately didn't grade answers and allowed unlimited retries: low stakes help, but it had no follow-ups.
- Some commercial JD-based tools (for example Huru) score answers 0–100 across several dimensions and call the scores coaching signals, not hiring decisions. This skill adds the user's quotes as evidence for every rating and keeps one priority.
- Common failures of generic chatbot mock interviewers: stacking several questions per turn, never following up, inflated praise, revealing scores every turn, model answers with invented metrics, ignoring level, and forgetting between sessions. This skill's rules target each one.
- Live "interview copilot" products exist and are marketed as undetectable. This skill does not provide live help.
