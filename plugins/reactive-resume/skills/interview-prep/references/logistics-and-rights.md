## 1. Day-of checklist

**When the invite arrives**

- Date, time and timezone. Convert to the user's own timezone and say both ("10:00 Berlin time, 09:00 yours").
- Format (video, phone, onsite), platform and link, or address; length; the names and roles of the interviewers.
- Round type and audience. With MCP, log it with `add_application_interview` (see mcp-recipes.md).
- The employer's AI and notes policy (section 2), and whether a coding tool, whiteboard or shared document is used.
- Any accommodation requested now, not on the day (section 3).
- Add "What are the next steps?" to the questions to ask.

**The day before**

- Test the exact platform link, camera, microphone and screen share; install any app or extension it needs.
- Have the interviewer's or recruiter's phone number or email for a dropout.
- For coding rounds, one practice run in their environment: font size, language, run shortcut.
- Print or save the resume version the employer received.
- Prepare a sticky note: 3–5 story titles and 3 questions. Never a script.
- Onsite: route, travel buffer, parking, ID, who to ask for at reception.

**The hour before**

- Room: quiet, light in front of the user rather than behind, plain background, camera at eye level.
- Phone silenced, notifications off, other apps closed (screen sharing exposes them).
- Network: wired or near the router; a phone hotspot as backup.
- Water. Say the opener aloud once. The nerves routine in section 5.

**Afterwards**

- Debrief within a day while recall is fresh (main workflow, step 11).
- Thank-you note if the user wants one: short, specific to each interviewer, within a day. It is a strong norm in the US and optional in much of the UK and EU; there is no good evidence it changes outcomes.
- Note their decision date and set a follow-up for two business days after it. In Ontario, employers with 25 or more employees must tell interviewed applicants for publicly advertised jobs, within 45 days of the last interview, whether a hiring decision has been made (as of 2026).

## 2. Asking about AI tools and notes

Policies vary by employer and sometimes by round. If the invitation doesn't say, draft one line for the recruiter:

> "Ahead of the technical round, could you confirm whether AI coding assistants or notes are permitted, and if so, which tools?"

Default to no AI help during live or timed assessments unless the employer says otherwise. See rounds.md for published employer policies.

## 3. Accommodations

- **United States (EEOC).** Ask orally or in writing, at any point, as early as possible; a family member or job coach can ask on the user's behalf. If the need isn't obvious, the employer may ask for reasonable documentation. The employer can choose among effective options and can't refuse to consider someone because they need an accommodation. Examples: large-print, braille or audio materials; interpreters; an accessible room; screen readers; extra time on tests. Employers may ask whether a candidate needs an accommodation for the hiring process.
- **United Kingdom.** Changing the recruitment process is a reasonable adjustment under the Equality Act. Access to Work can fund a BSL interpreter or other communication support at a job interview.
- **One-way video platforms.** Accommodations usually go through the recruiter; HireVue says neurodivergent candidates may be able to get questions in advance.
- **Disclosure is the user's choice.** They can request the adjustment without naming a diagnosis. Offer the trade-offs; don't push either way.

Template:

> "I'm looking forward to the [round] on [date]. To take part on equal terms, I'm requesting [specific adjustment, e.g. 25% extra time on the coding exercise / questions in writing as well as spoken / captions enabled]. Happy to discuss what works for your process."

## 4. Unlawful or inappropriate questions

| Jurisdiction            | Key rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Source                                           |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| United States (federal) | Disability questions before an offer are prohibited. Questions about race, sex, religion, national origin or age aren't banned outright but can be evidence of intent to discriminate; the same goes for marital status, children, childcare, pregnancy and a spouse's job. Employers may ask whether the candidate can perform the job's functions with or without reasonable accommodation | EEOC [O]                                         |
| United States, deadline | An EEOC charge is due within 180 days, or 300 where a state or local agency enforces a similar law                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | EEOC [O]                                         |
| United Kingdom          | Employers shouldn't ask about protected characteristics, marital or civil-partnership status, or children and plans for children. Health questions only for intrinsic job requirements, interview adjustments or positive action. Date of birth only where a minimum age applies. Spent convictions need not be disclosed, with exceptions (e.g. work with children)                         | gov.uk [O]                                       |
| Germany                 | Questions about pregnancy, religion, family planning, or union or party membership are generally inadmissible; AGG discrimination claims have a two-month written-notice window                                                                                                                                                                                                              | [unverified; check an official German source]    |
| Canada, Australia       | Human-rights law bars discriminatory questions; employers should ask only about genuine job requirements                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | [unverified; check the provincial or state body] |
| Elsewhere (e.g. India)  | Private-sector protections are narrower. Describe a question as "not job-related" rather than "illegal"                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | [practitioner]                                   |

**Response playbook.** Offer these options; the user chooses:

1. **Answer** if comfortable.
2. **Answer the underlying concern:** "If you're asking about my availability, I can commit to the hours and travel in the posting."
3. **Redirect:** "I'd like to keep the focus on what I'd bring to the role. For example…"
4. **Decline politely:** "I don't think that's relevant to my ability to do this job."
5. **Note it and escalate later:** write down the date, the interviewer and the exact words, then decide whether to raise it with the recruiter or HR or file a complaint. Give the official link and deadline; no legal conclusions.

In every jurisdiction, suggest declining or redirecting rather than answering untruthfully. If the user asks about German case law on inadmissible questions, point them to an official source or an employment lawyer; don't advise it.

## 5. Nerves: evidence-graded techniques

| Technique                                               | Evidence                                                                                                                                    | How to use it                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Know what anxiety actually does                         | A meta-analysis found a modest negative relation between interview anxiety and performance                                                  | Normalise it: it costs a little, it doesn't decide the outcome                   |
| Practise aloud, including a full mock round             | Coaching and preparation were linked to better, more organised interview answers (Maurer et al. 2001)                                       | At least one full mock per round type (mock-interview)                           |
| Reappraise arousal as excitement ("I'm excited")        | Outperformed trying to calm down in small lab studies of speaking, singing and maths (Brooks 2014)                                          | 30 seconds before the call                                                       |
| Slow breathing with long exhales (cyclic sighing)       | A randomised trial found 5 minutes a day improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness; not tested right before interviews | 2–5 minutes beforehand                                                           |
| Expressive writing: 10 minutes writing the worries down | Improved exam scores for anxious students (Ramirez & Beilock 2011); replication record mixed                                                | Optional, the night before or an hour before                                     |
| Recovery lines                                          | Practitioner advice                                                                                                                         | "Can I take a moment to structure that?" / "Let me restart that more concisely." |
| Power posing                                            | Hormone and behaviour effects failed to replicate (Ranehill et al. 2015)                                                                    | Don't recommend                                                                  |

A shaky first minute is recoverable: research on interviewer decision time finds it varies with interviewer experience, rapport and how structured the interview is, and no study supports "they decide in 90 seconds".
