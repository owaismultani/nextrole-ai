## 1. Turning JD lines into questions

Formula: take a must-have, name the behaviour behind it, and ask for one past instance with a constraint that makes it real.

| JD line                                      | Competency                    | Lead question                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "Partner with Product to define KPIs"        | Influence, analytical         | "Tell me about a metric you got a product team to adopt. How did you get them to agree?"               |
| "Thrive in ambiguity"                        | Ambiguity                     | "Tell me about a project that started with an unclear goal. How did you decide what done looked like?" |
| "Own services end to end, including on-call" | Ownership, incident judgement | "Walk me through the worst incident you handled. What did you do in the first hour?"                   |
| "Manage a team of 6–8"                       | People leadership             | "Tell me about someone on your team who was struggling. What did you do, and how did it end?"          |
| "Excellent written communication"            | Communication                 | "Tell me about a document you wrote that changed a decision. Who was it for?"                          |

Each lead gets 2–3 planned probes from section 6 and four anchors (see scoring.md).

## 2. Lead questions and probes by competency

| Competency                  | Lead questions                                                                                        | Planned probes                                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ownership and delivery      | A time you delivered despite a blocker you didn't control. A project you would scope differently now. | What was yours to decide? What did you cut? How did it land?                                             |
| Conflict                    | A disagreement with a peer or manager about direction. Two teams that wanted opposite things.         | What did they want and why? What did you do first? Where did you end up?                                 |
| Influence without authority | Persuading someone senior, or a team you didn't manage, to change course.                             | What objection did you hear? What changed their mind? What would you do with less time?                  |
| Failure and learning        | A decision that turned out wrong. A deadline you missed.                                              | When did you realise? What did you do next? What do you do differently now?                              |
| Ambiguity                   | Starting work with unclear goals or owners.                                                           | How did you define done? Who did you check with? What did you assume?                                    |
| Prioritisation              | Too many requests and not enough time. Saying no to a stakeholder.                                    | How did you rank them? Who lost out? How did you tell them?                                              |
| Data and judgement          | A decision where the data and your instinct disagreed.                                                | What did the data show? What did you do? How did it turn out?                                            |
| Customer focus              | Going against an internal preference for a customer's sake.                                           | How did you know what the customer needed? Who pushed back? What happened to the customer?               |
| Learning fast               | Getting productive in an unfamiliar tool, domain or team quickly.                                     | What did you do in the first week? How did you know you were up to speed?                                |
| Quality and standards       | Raising the bar on something that was "good enough" for others.                                       | What was wrong with it? What did it cost? Did the change stick?                                          |
| Simplifying                 | Removing a step, a tool or a process.                                                                 | Who depended on it? How did you check nothing broke?                                                     |
| Communication               | Explaining something technical to a non-technical audience. Delivering bad news.                      | How did you know they understood? What did you leave out?                                                |
| People leadership (manager) | A performance problem you handled. Building or reshaping a team. A hire you got wrong.                | What did you say, and when? What support did you give? What was the outcome for the person and the team? |
| Ethics and integrity        | Being asked to cut a corner.                                                                          | What exactly were you asked? What did you do? What did it cost you?                                      |

## 3. Recruiter staples

| Question                                        | A good answer covers                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Walk me through your background."              | Present role and scope, one or two proof points, the thread that leads to this role; 60–120 s                  |
| "Why are you looking now?"                      | A forward reason; no complaints about the current employer                                                     |
| "Why this role / company?"                      | Something specific from the JD or the company's work, linked to the user's experience                          |
| "What are you looking for next?"                | Matches the role; honest about must-haves (location, scope)                                                    |
| Logistics (location, notice, authorization)     | Direct, accurate answers                                                                                       |
| "Salary expectations?"                          | A researched range, or a polite request to hear the band first; never a number the user can't live with        |
| "Other processes?"                              | Honest and brief; no invented competing offers                                                                 |
| "Why did you leave?" / "Tell me about this gap" | Brief, neutral, forward-looking and true; never deny a layoff or a dismissal, and never blame the old employer |

## 4. Hiring-manager staples

- "What would you focus on in your first 90 days?"
- "Tell me about the project on your resume you're proudest of."
- "How do you like to be managed? How do you like to give and receive feedback?"
- "What kind of work drains you?"
- A situational question drawn from the JD's hardest responsibility: "Two weeks before launch, a key partner says they can't deliver. Walk me through what you'd do."

## 5. Resume deep-dives

Pick the line with the biggest claim or the strongest match to a must-have.

- "Your resume says you [claim]. Walk me through how that happened."
- "What was the situation before you started?"
- "Which part of that was yours, and which was the team's?"
- "How was [number] measured? Who measured it?"
- "What would have happened if you hadn't done it?"

If the user can't support a resume claim, note it kindly in the report and suggest resume-tailor or resume-bullet-writer to fix the line before the real interview.

## 6. Planned probe bank

Adapted from the US Office of Personnel Management's structured-interview probe list.

- **Situation:** "What led up to it?" "What was the most critical issue?" "Who else was involved?"
- **Action:** "What did you do first?" "What did you weigh before acting?" "What did you decide against?" "What did you say to them?"
- **Outcome:** "How did it turn out?" "How did you measure that?" "What would you do differently?" "Where have you used that since?"
- **Ownership:** "What was your part versus the team's?" "Whose call was that?"

Prefer probes about checkable specifics (sequence, systems, numbers, who said what). Open-ended "tell me more" invites embellishment.

## 7. Curveballs for Hard

- "Give me a second example."
- "What would your harshest critic say about that decision?"
- "Tell me about a time this approach didn't work."
- "If you had half the time, what would you have cut?"
- "Your manager disagreed. Why were they wrong, or were they right?"
- "That sounds like the team's success. What would have gone differently without you?"

Use one or two per session; more feels like hostility rather than rigour.

## 8. Amazon Leadership Principles

Amazon lists 16 principles (as of 2026; check amazon.jobs for the current wording). Tag each question with one principle; most real loops split them across interviewers.

| Principle                                    | Probe theme                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer Obsession                           | Working backwards from a customer need              |
| Ownership                                    | Acting beyond your remit; long-term over short-term |
| Invent and Simplify                          | A new approach, or removing complexity              |
| Are Right, A Lot                             | Judgement with incomplete data; changing your mind  |
| Learn and Be Curious                         | Learning something new and using it                 |
| Hire and Develop the Best                    | Hiring, coaching, raising others                    |
| Insist on the Highest Standards              | Refusing to ship something not good enough          |
| Think Big                                    | A bold direction others hadn't considered           |
| Bias for Action                              | A calculated risk taken quickly                     |
| Frugality                                    | Doing more with less                                |
| Earn Trust                                   | Candid feedback; admitting a mistake                |
| Dive Deep                                    | Finding a root cause in the data                    |
| Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit           | Disagreeing openly, then committing                 |
| Deliver Results                              | Delivering despite setbacks                         |
| Strive to be Earth's Best Employer           | Making work better for a team                       |
| Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | Considering wider effects of a decision             |

## 9. Judging the user's questions for the interviewer

Strong questions are specific to the role or team, show the user read the JD, and help them decide (what success looks like at 6 months, the team's biggest current problem, how decisions get made, what the last person in the role moved on to). Weak ones are answered in the JD already, are generic ("What's the culture like?" with no follow-up), or only concern perks. Answer them generically in character and add a `[Coach]` note that you can't speak for the company. In the report, rate them in one line and suggest one better question. With MCP, offer to save good ones to the workspace.

## 10. Awkward-question drill (opt-in)

Run only when the user asks. Ask one improper question in character, then step out and coach. Examples of questions that are improper in many jurisdictions (US EEOC guidance limits pre-employment questions to job-relevant information, bars disability questions before an offer, and discourages asking for photos):

- "Do you have kids, or plan to?"
- "Where are you originally from?"
- "How old are you?"
- "Do you have any health conditions we should know about?"

Coach a calm redirect that answers the legitimate concern behind the question: "If you're asking whether I can meet the schedule, yes: I can do the on-call rota and travel twice a month." The user decides how much to share; they don't have to answer, and they can note the question for later. This is not legal advice, and rules vary by country.
