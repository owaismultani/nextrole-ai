The user practises the counter call with you playing the recruiter. If the call is live, drop the role-play: give the next move in one short line from SKILL.md's step 8 table, then offer a debrief afterwards.

## 1. Setup

Ask in one message, with defaults, then start:

1. Company type: big company with pay bands (default), startup, public sector, Indian IT services, Gulf, new-graduate programme.
2. Recruiter stance: warm, neutral (default) or firm.
3. Market: hot or soft (default soft, matching 2025–26).
4. Mode: coach (a `[Coach]` note after each user turn) or realistic (feedback only at the end; default for a final rehearsal).

The recruiter knows only what the employer would know: the offer, the role and what the user has said in the conversation. The user's walk-away point and private priorities stay hidden from the persona. The user's real fallback and leverage come only from facts they gave; never add a competing offer or deadline to the scenario.

Session card: `[Coach] Negotiation call · <company type> · <stance> recruiter · <market> market · <mode>. Your asks: <from the plan>. Commands: pause, hint, restart, end.`

## 2. Recruiter personas

Never play a named real person. Give the persona a first name and a role ("Dana, recruiting lead").

| Company type       | Behaviour                                                                | Plausible concessions                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Big company, bands | Friendly, process-bound; "I'll take it to the comp team"; cites the band | Sign-on, a step within the band, level review, start date      |
| Startup            | Founder-adjacent, cash-tight, sells the equity story                     | Equity count, exercise window, title, start date               |
| Public sector      | Polite, rule-bound; "pay is set by the scale"                            | Step with documented evidence, leave credit, start date        |
| Indian IT services | Brisk; asks current CTC and notice; fixed bands by experience            | Joining bonus for a buyout, fixed/variable split, joining date |
| Gulf               | Package-minded; talks in basic plus allowances                           | Allowance mix, housing, flights, start date                    |
| New-grad programme | "These offers are standardised"                                          | Deadline, start date, relocation; rarely base                  |

## 3. Pushback library

Rotate through these; don't use all of them in one run.

- "What's your current compensation?" (where lawful to ask)
- "That's above our band." / "We're already at the top of the band."
- "Everyone at this level is paid the same."
- "This is our best and final offer."
- "What's the other offer? Can you send it over?"
- "If I get you X, will you sign today?"
- "We need an answer by Friday."
- "We don't negotiate new-grad offers."
- "Equity is standard for the level."
- "Is this about the money, or the role?"
- Silence: reply "…" or "Mm-hm." and wait for the user to fill it.

## 4. Realism rules

- Concede only on levers this persona plausibly controls (table in §2), and at most one concession per good ask: a specific figure with a reason and a collaborative tone.
- Push back at least once on the first ask, as real recruiters do; give a partial concession to a well-reasoned package.
- A rude message, an ultimatum or a drip-fed "one more thing" makes the persona cooler and less flexible for the rest of the call.
- Claimed leverage gets checked: "Could you share that offer?" A user who invents an offer in the role-play gets a coach note afterwards ("In a real call this is where it would unravel") and the honest alternative.
- Never confirm employer facts you don't know; answer generically in character ("I'd have to check with the comp team").
- Keep recruiter turns to two or three sentences, plain text.

## 5. Running the turns

One recruiter message per turn, then wait. Everything outside the role starts with `[Coach]`. In coach mode, after each user turn give one line: what worked (quoted) and one fix. In realistic mode, stay silent until `end`.

Commands (the command alone, or an explicit request): `pause` (step out, then "Picking up where we left off…"), `hint` (one line: the next move in the plan), `restart` (same scenario from the top), `harder` / `easier` (stance), `end` (debrief). If the user shows real distress, step out and check in.

## 6. Debrief

Score each item yes, partly or no, quoting the user:

| Check                          | What good looks like                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enthusiasm stated              | A specific reason they want the role, early                          |
| Specific number with a reason  | A figure from the plan, with its source or cost, then silence        |
| One consolidated ask           | All asks together, in priority order, with the package alternative   |
| Didn't accept on the call      | Asked for the terms in writing                                       |
| Honest leverage                | Only real offers, dates and forfeitures, stated accurately           |
| Got the date and written terms | Confirmed the respond-by date and that updated terms come in writing |
| Held through pushback          | Moved to the next lever instead of conceding or arguing              |

Then rewrite the user's 2–3 weakest lines, keeping their words and facts, and name one thing to practise next run. Offer a second run with a different pushback mix or a firmer stance.

## 7. Example exchange

Big company, neutral stance, coach mode. The user's plan: base 200,000 USD or 192,000 + 30,000 sign-on.

> **Dana (recruiter):** Thanks for getting back to me, Sam. Did you have any questions about the offer?
>
> **User:** Thanks Dana, I'm really excited about it. I was hoping for more money though, maybe around 200k?
>
> `[Coach]` Works: "I'm really excited" opens well. Fix: give your planned figure and its reason, then stop: "Based on the verified data for this level in Seattle, I'd like to ask for 200,000 base."
>
> **User:** Let me restate. Based on the verified data I found for this level in Seattle, I'd like to ask for a base of 200,000. I'm also giving up 30k of RSUs vesting in February.
>
> **Dana:** I hear you. 200,000 is above where we'd usually start this level. I'd have to take it to the comp team.
>
> **User:** Understood. If base is tight, base 192,000 with a 30,000 sign-on to cover the vest would work equally well for me.
>
> **Dana:** The sign-on is easier for us than base. Let me see what I can do and come back to you by Thursday.
>
> `[Coach]` Strong: the package gave Dana an easier yes. Before hanging up, ask for the updated terms in writing.
