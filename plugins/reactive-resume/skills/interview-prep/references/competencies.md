## 1. Choosing a framework

1. If the employer publishes the criteria (values page, competency framework, interview prep page, recruiter email), use those and cite the URL with the date you read it.
2. If the employer is one of those below, load that section, and treat anything marked **reported** as unconfirmed: check the employer's current careers pages or ask the recruiter.
3. Otherwise use the generic core, weighted by the posting's must-haves: a must-have the posting repeats gets two stories.
4. Frameworks change. Re-check the employer's page when the interview matters and you rely on an exact list.

## 2. Generic core with example questions

| Competency                    | Example questions                                                                            |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ownership and accountability  | A time you took on something outside your role. A problem you saw that nobody was fixing.    |
| Influence without authority   | A time you convinced someone senior. Getting buy-in from people who didn't report to you.    |
| Collaboration                 | A project that depended on another team. How did you keep it moving?                         |
| Conflict and disagreement     | A disagreement with your manager. A difficult peer. A time you were wrong in a disagreement. |
| Ambiguity and prioritisation  | A project with unclear goals. Too many priorities at once: what did you drop?                |
| Customer focus                | Going beyond for a customer. A time you said no to one.                                      |
| Results under pressure        | Your most significant achievement. Delivering with far fewer resources than you wanted.      |
| Data-driven judgement         | A decision you changed because of data. A time the data and the anecdote disagreed.          |
| Learning and growth           | Learning a new domain fast. Feedback that changed how you work.                              |
| Communication                 | Explaining something complex to a non-expert. A message that landed badly and what you did.  |

| Adaptability                  | A time the plan changed under you. How you handled a reorganisation or a new manager.        |

## 3. Amazon Leadership Principles

Verified on amazon.jobs on 2026-10-09: 16 principles. Amazon's interview pages name behavioural questions, the STAR method, the Leadership Principles and Bar Raisers (an interviewer from outside the hiring team) as part of the process, and amazon.jobs publishes role-specific prep pages. Ask the recruiter for the prep material for the specific loop.

| Principle                                    | What a story should show                                               | Example question                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer Obsession                           | Started from the customer and worked backwards; earned their trust     | A time you went beyond for a customer. A time you said no to one.     |
| Ownership                                    | Acted for the long term; owned something beyond the user's own scope   | A time you took on something outside your area.                       |
| Invent and Simplify                          | A new or simpler way, adopted                                          | A process you simplified.                                             |
| Are Right, A Lot                             | Good judgement; sought out other views; changed their mind on evidence | A decision with incomplete data. A time you were wrong.               |
| Learn and Be Curious                         | Learned fast; explored beyond the role                                 | Something you taught yourself recently, and why.                      |
| Hire and Develop the Best                    | Raised the bar in hiring; developed people                             | Someone you coached who now outperforms. A hiring call you'd change.  |
| Insist on the Highest Standards              | Refused to accept a defect; raised a team's bar                        | A time you wouldn't ship.                                             |
| Think Big                                    | A bold direction and how they brought people along                     | The most ambitious thing you've driven.                               |
| Bias for Action                              | Moved fast on a reversible decision; calculated risk                   | A decision you made quickly without all the data.                     |
| Frugality                                    | Did more with less; constraints breeding invention                     | Delivering with far fewer resources than you wanted.                  |
| Earn Trust                                   | Listened, spoke candidly, admitted mistakes                            | A time you had to rebuild trust.                                      |
| Dive Deep                                    | Stayed close to details; audited; found the root cause                 | A time data and anecdote disagreed. A problem you traced to its root. |
| Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit           | Challenged a decision respectfully with evidence, then committed fully | A time you disagreed with your manager and what happened.             |
| Deliver Results                              | Hit the key inputs on time despite setbacks                            | Your most significant result. A time you nearly missed a deadline.    |
| Strive to be Earth's Best Employer           | Made the work environment better, safer or fairer for people           | Something you changed to make your team's work better.                |
| Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | Considered second-order effects on the wider community or business     | A time you weighed the wider impact of a decision.                    |

Coverage (practitioner heuristics, not Amazon rules): at most 3 primary competency tags per story, framework tags included; aim for at least one story per principle; prepare two each for Deliver Results, Ownership, Customer Obsession, Dive Deep and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, which come up often. Practitioners report that each loop interviewer covers 2–3 principles; prepare different stories for different interviewers where possible.

Senior candidates: expect scope questions (multi-team, multi-quarter), and stories for Think Big, Are Right A Lot and Hire and Develop the Best. Product-management loops also commonly include product and metrics questions alongside the principles [practitioner reports; confirm with the recruiter]: see rounds.md for product sense and execution rounds. Expect detailed follow-ups on numbers (Dive Deep), so every Result needs its source.

For PM loops, have the user ask the recruiter (1) which Leadership Principles each interviewer covers, (2) whether there is a writing exercise, and (3) which slot is the Bar Raiser. Prepare one story where the user started from the customer and worked backwards (a PR/FAQ or similar document, if they have written one), and be ready to talk about reversible versus irreversible decisions for Bias for Action [practitioner reports; confirm with the recruiter].

## 4. Google (reported)

Google has long described four attributes; the current careers pages could not be verified for this skill, so confirm before relying on them: general cognitive ability (structured problem solving, often through hypothetical "how would you…" questions), role-related knowledge, leadership (emergent: stepping up when needed and stepping back), and "Googleyness" (comfort with ambiguity, bias to action, collaboration, intellectual humility). Prepare the hypothetical method in section 10 as well as stories.

## 5. Meta (reported)

Widely reported behavioural signals, not verified on Meta's pages: resolving conflict, growing continuously, embracing ambiguity, driving results, communicating effectively. Map one or two stories to each, and confirm with the recruiter's prep material.

## 6. Consulting fit: McKinsey, BCG, Bain

- **McKinsey Personal Experience Interview (reported; confirm on McKinsey's current prep pages).** One story per dimension, probed for 10–15 minutes. Recent dimensions are reported as Personal Impact, Entrepreneurial Drive, Inclusive Leadership and Courageous Change; older materials list Leadership, Personal Impact and Entrepreneurial Drive. Use depth mode from story-bank.md: what was said, the reasoning at each step, and the tension involved.
- **Bain** says it uses behavioural interviews, cases (or coding for some roles), portfolio reviews for design roles and sometimes a written assignment of about three days ending in a presentation, asks the same questions of all candidates for a role, and asks candidates to discuss its operating principles. Prepare one story per published principle.
- **BCG** publishes case-interview preparation (structure, asking questions, analysing data, quick maths, no single right answer). Fit questions follow the generic core.

## 7. UK Civil Service Success Profiles

Verified on gov.uk (candidate overview updated January 2025). Five elements: Ability, Technical, Behaviours, Strengths, Experience. The job advert says which are assessed.

**Nine behaviours**, specific to grade, with only some assessed for any one role: Seeing the big picture; Changing and improving; Making effective decisions; Leadership; Communicating and influencing; Working together; Developing self and others; Managing a quality service; Delivering at pace.

- STAR is recommended. Make the link to the behaviour explicit, and don't assume the panel understands your context.
- Written behaviour statements have word limits set in the advert; use the written-example format in story-bank.md.
- **Strengths questions** are quick questions about what energises the candidate and what they do well naturally ("What do you enjoy most about…?"). They are not STAR answers. The Civil Service tells candidates to reflect on their strengths beforehand, so prepare by listing tasks the user finds energising or draining, with a short example of each.

## 8. NHS values

The NHS Constitution's six values: Working together for patients; Respect and dignity; Commitment to quality of care; Compassion; Improving lives; Everyone counts. NHS employers use values-based recruitment: values interviews, role plays, written scenario responses and assessment centres. Map at least one story to each value, and for clinical roles prepare scenario answers (prioritisation, escalation using SBAR) from rounds.md.

## 9. A company's own published values

1. Fetch the values or "how we work" page and note the URL and date.
2. Map each value to the nearest generic-core competency.
3. Assign one story per value; mark empty rows for mining.
4. In answers, show the value through what the user did. Reciting the value back ("I'm very customer-obsessed") without evidence scores nothing.

## 10. Hypothetical and situational questions

"What would you do if…" questions test reasoning. Answer with a method, then anchor it in a real story if one exists:

1. Clarify the goal and constraints (ask one or two questions, or state assumptions).
2. Lay out two or three options and the trade-offs.
3. Choose one and say why.
4. Say how you'd know it was working and what would make you change course.
5. Anchor: "In fact, when I faced something similar at X, I…" only when the user has a real matching story.

Practise the method aloud on 2–3 likely hypotheticals drawn from the posting.
