Build every answer from the story bank and the resume the interviewer received. Draft speaking notes (4–6 bullets), not scripts: memorised word-for-word answers sound read aloud and break when the question is phrased differently. Examples below show structure; every specific in a real answer comes from the user.

## 1. Tell me about yourself

Structure: **present → past → future** (popularised by career coach Lily Zhang).

1. **Present:** current role, scope, one recent win.
2. **Past:** 1–2 earlier proof points relevant to this posting.
3. **Future:** why this role is the next step.

Length: there is no proven ideal; advice ranges from 30 seconds to about 2 minutes. Default to 60–90 seconds. Tune the detail to the audience: a recruiter wants the fit and the motivation; a hiring manager wants the relevant proof; an executive wants scope and direction.

Don't: recite the resume in date order, open with a layoff or firing, criticise an employer, or memorise it word for word.

- **Before** (chronological, 4 minutes): "I studied computer science at X, graduated in 2016, then joined Y as a junior developer, where I worked on… then in 2019…"
- **After** (structure only; every bracket comes from the user): "I lead [area] at [company]. Last quarter [result, with the user's number or range]. Before that I spent [N] years [proof point matching the posting], which is why [cited fact from the brief] caught my eye. I'm looking for [what this role offers]."

## 2. Why us, why this role

Formula: **a specific company fact (cited in the brief) + the user's matching evidence + what they would contribute.** Require at least two facts that a candidate who only read the homepage couldn't use.

- **Before:** "You're an industry leader and I love your culture."
- **After** (structure): "In your Q2 call you said self-serve onboarding is the growth priority. I rebuilt onboarding at Z and lifted activation by [the user's real figure or range]. Your engineering blog's post on the event pipeline describes the stack I'd be working in, and I've run that pattern in production."

If the user can't name anything that draws them to the company, say so privately: the answer will sound generic, and the brief needs more work before the interview.

## 3. Weakness

Rules:

- A **real** weakness that is **not a core requirement** of this posting. Check it against the must-haves; if it hits one, pick another.
- Show the **mechanism** adopted and the **evidence** of progress, and admit what is still in progress.
- No humblebrags ("perfectionism", "I work too hard"): they read as evasive.

Template: "Earlier in my career I [behaviour] → it cost [impact] → since [when] I [mechanism] → [evidence of change] → I'm still working on [edge]."

- **Before:** "I'm a perfectionist."
- **After** (structure only; every bracket comes from the user): "I used to [behaviour]; [when], it cost [the user's real impact]. Since then I [mechanism]. [Evidence of change, in the user's words]. I'm still working on [edge]."

## 4. Failure

Pick a real failure with real impact. Own the user's part in the first sentence; then the recovery; then the Reflection; then the system change. Never present a disguised success ("my failure was caring too much"). Ask the user what their part was before writing it, rather than assigning blame yourself.

## 5. Conflict

Frame the disagreement as being about the work, not personalities. Show that the user listened (restate the other view fairly), what evidence they brought, how it resolved (agreement, escalation, or disagree-and-commit), and what the working relationship was like afterwards. No villain: the interviewer is picturing working with the user.

## 6. Gaps, layoffs, termination, short tenure, why leaving

| Situation     | Shape                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Career gap    | One factual sentence, optionally "during that time I…", then "I'm ready for X now because…". No apology. Health and family details are the user's choice and rarely needed |
| Layoff        | Neutral scope: "my role was one of [N] cut in the [month year] restructure." It isn't a performance matter, so don't defend it as one. Then what they did next             |
| Termination   | Brief and honest, what they learned, no blame. Rehearse until it stays under 30 seconds                                                                                    |
| Short tenure  | The true reason in one line (reorganisation, role changed, relocation), what they delivered anyway, why this role is a better fit for the long term                        |
| Why leaving   | Pull toward this role rather than push away from the current one                                                                                                           |
| Career change | The bridging story: why the move, what they've done to prepare (with dates), the overlap with this role                                                                    |

Never invent a softer reason for a departure. If the user is unsure what a reference check would show, suggest they ask the former employer or check their records.

## 7. Salary expectations and pay history

**Step 1: read the posting.** Pay ranges in postings are common where law requires them. For example, Ontario requires employers with 25 or more employees to include the expected pay or a range in public postings from 1 January 2026 (the range may be no wider than $50,000, and roles over $200,000 are exempt); postings must also disclose AI use in screening, and employers must tell each interviewed applicant, within 45 days of their last interview, whether a hiring decision has been made for the posting (not necessarily the outcome for them).

**Step 2: check the jurisdiction for pay-history questions** (as of 2026; laws change, so verify locally before relying on this):

| Jurisdiction     | Pay-history question                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| United States    | No federal ban. A state and city patchwork: HR Dive's April 2026 update counted 22 state-wide and 24 local bans, including California, Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Washington and DC, and cities such as New York City and Philadelphia. Virginia's ban extended to all employers on 1 July 2026. California employers may ask about expectations and must give the pay scale on reasonable request |
| European Union   | Pay Transparency Directive 2023/970, Article 5: where transposed, applicants get the starting pay or range before the interview (or otherwise in time to negotiate), and employers may not ask about pay history. The transposition deadline was 7 June 2026; national laws vary, so check the member state                                                                                                                                                     |
| Ontario          | Posting rules above; no pay-history ban confirmed on the province's guide                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| British Columbia | Pay-history questions banned since May 2023; ranges required in public postings since 1 November 2023                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| UK, much of Asia | No statutory ban                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| India            | Current and expected CTC are routinely asked and lawful; the user decides how to answer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

**Scripts:**

- **Expectations:** "Based on the posted range and comparable roles, I'm targeting [X–Y] base, depending on the full package and scope. What band is budgeted for this role?" The range comes from the user's research and starts at their target, not their walk-away figure; with weak data, ask for the band or defer instead. Never invent market figures.
- **History where banned:** "I'd rather focus on what this role is worth. My expectation is [X–Y]."
- **History where lawful:** declining is still allowed, with the same pivot. If the user chooses to share, state it plainly.
- **Before:** "I'm currently on 85k." **After:** "I'd rather not anchor on my current pay. Can you share the range budgeted for this role? From my research, roles like this pay [X–Y], and I'm flexible within that depending on the full package."

"Whoever names a number first loses" is contested: first offers can anchor, but a posted range often anchors the conversation anyway. Treat it as context-dependent. For choosing the number in depth or negotiating an offer, hand off to offer-negotiation if installed (its Expectations mode covers the number before any offer); otherwise job-application-manager's offer section. Keep currencies and pay periods as given; never convert with made-up rates.

## 8. Length, "I" vs "we", and delivery

- **Length:** 90–120 seconds per behavioural answer (Indeed suggests 1.5–2 minutes; LinkedIn's interviewer guide 2–3). At typical speaking pace that is about 200–300 words. Depth rounds: a 2-minute headline, then the probe layer.
- **"I" vs "we":** "I" for the user's actions; "we" only for team context and shared outcomes. MIT's careers office notes that "we" makes the individual contribution hard to see.
- **Delivery:** answer the question asked in the first sentence, then the story. If an answer starts to sprawl, "Let me restart that more concisely" is fine. "Can I take a moment to structure that?" buys thinking time.

## 9. Closing the interview

- Ask 2–3 prepared questions for this interviewer (see the main workflow, step 8).
- Ask: "Is there anything about my background you'd like me to clarify?" It surfaces an objection while the user can still answer it.
- Ask: "What are the next steps, and when should I expect to hear?" Log the answer as the follow-up date.

## 10. Myths not to repeat

| Myth                                                                 | What the evidence says                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "Interviewers decide in the first 7 / 30 / 90 seconds"               | No traceable study. Decision speed varies with interviewer experience, rapport and structure. The first minute sets tone; a shaky start is recoverable |
| "Communication is 7% words, 38% tone, 55% body language"             | Mehrabian says it applies only to messages about feelings and attitudes, not interview answers                                                         |
| "Power pose before you go in"                                        | The hormone and behaviour effects failed to replicate. Don't recommend it                                                                              |
| "Prepare exactly 8–12 stories" / "STAR must be 20/10/60/10"          | Practitioner heuristics. Size by coverage; the time split is a rough guide                                                                             |
| "Amazon has 14 Leadership Principles"                                | Out of date: amazon.jobs lists 16                                                                                                                      |
| "Use 'we' to show you're a team player"                              | Interviewer guides reward "I" for your actions; "we" hides your contribution                                                                           |
| "Glassdoor questions are what you'll get"                            | Self-selected reports, and structured question sets rotate. Use them as themes                                                                         |
| "Video interview AI reads your face; keep eye contact with the lens" | HireVue dropped facial analysis in 2020 and scores mainly the transcribed answers. Coach content and structure                                         |
| "You can't prepare for strengths-based interviews"                   | The UK Civil Service tells candidates to reflect on their strengths beforehand                                                                         |
| "STAR alone is enough"                                               | Structured interviews probe what led up to it, what you did first and what you'd change. Prepare the probe layer and Reflection                        |
| "Say 'perfectionism' as your weakness"                               | Reads as evasive. Pick a real one that isn't a core requirement                                                                                        |
| Thank-you note statistics ("X% of managers won't hire…")             | Old staffing-firm polls with thin methods. A courtesy, not a deciding factor                                                                           |
| "Whoever names a number first loses"                                 | Contested: first offers can anchor, but a posted range often anchors the conversation anyway (section 7)                                               |
