## 1. What the research supports

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Rule in this skill                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pew (2023, n = 5,188 US workers): 32% of men and 28% of women asked for more than the first offer. Of those who asked, 28% got what they asked for, 38% got more but less than asked, 35% got the original offer. Women were likelier to get only the original (38% vs 31%).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Negotiating is normal and usually yields something; never promise it.                                                                              |
| Field experiments with over 3,100 US tech job seekers recruited via Levels.fyi: a light encouragement message raised the share who countered (61% vs 54%) and raised compensation gains; candidates who countered gained about 12% on average (a descriptive figure, not the effect of the message). An 80% coaching discount didn't change attempts (Cullen, Pakzad-Hurson & Perez-Truglia, NBER w33903, 2025).                                                                                                                | Encourage asking; don't oversell coaching.                                                                                                         |
| Candidates greatly overestimate rescission risk. Hiring managers reported making 26.9 offers on average and withdrawing 1.73 after the candidate negotiated, usually over rude or inappropriate behaviour (Hart, Bear & Ren, OBHDP 2024). Not a rate per negotiator.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | "Uncommon, not zero"; tone is the risk; no resigning before the signed offer.                                                                      |
| First offers anchor outcomes, but the effect disappears when the receiver focuses on their own target, alternatives or the other side's limit (Galinsky & Mussweiler, JPSP 2001).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Restate target, walk-away and fallback before reacting to their number.                                                                            |
| Ambitious first offers raised agreement value a lot (g = 1.14) but also impasses (g = −0.42, from 13 effects) and recipients' bad feeling (g = −0.40), driven by anger; effects shrank with more issues on the table (Petrowsky et al., OBHDP 2025 meta-analysis).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Anchor high but defensible, with a reason; negotiate several issues together.                                                                      |
| Moving first hurts when it reveals your priorities to a self-interested counterpart (Loschelder et al., J Appl Psych 2016).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Present packages, not "base matters most".                                                                                                         |
| Precise offers draw smaller counter-adjustments and read as researched (Mason et al., JESP 2013); excessive precision backfires with experts unless justified.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Precise figures only with a basis (a percentile, a calculation).                                                                                   |
| Ranges that start at the target and go up ("bolstering") beat a single number with no relationship cost; ranges that drop below the target lost value (Ames & Mason, JPSP 2015).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | A range's floor is the target, never the walk-away point.                                                                                          |
| Two or three equal-value packages offered at once set stronger anchors, read as flexible and sincere, and raised joint value (Leonardelli et al., OBHDP 2019).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Offer 2–3 packages of equal value to the user.                                                                                                     |
| Negotiators gained about $5,000 on average; trading cash for perks raised satisfaction (Marks & Harold, J Org Behav 2011, n = 149; correlational).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Trade across issues; non-cash terms count.                                                                                                         |
| Evaluators penalised women more than men for asking (Bowles, Babcock & Lai, OBHDP 2007). Reasons that show the ask is legitimate and relational improved women's outcomes in vignette studies (Bowles & Babcock, 2013). Saying pay is negotiable removed the gender gap in negotiating (Leibbrandt & List, 2015). The gap is small and shrinks with information and experience (Mazei et al., 2015; Kugler et al., 2018). Women ask as often as men but succeed less in Australian matched data (Artz, Goodall & Oswald, 2018). | Remove ambiguity first (ask for the range); give every user reasons plus collaborative phrasing; don't lecture; respect a choice not to negotiate. |
| A 2026 close replication of Amanatullah & Morris (2010) gave mixed results and didn't reproduce the "women negotiate better for others" interaction (FORRT).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Treat advocacy effects as context-dependent.                                                                                                       |
| Exploding offers push markets to early, thin matching (lab evidence, AEJ Micro 2009); NACE says students given enough time decide better and renege less.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Always ask once for reasonable time.                                                                                                               |
| In lab email negotiations over a simulated commercial deal, a short personal call beforehand helped; pairs who skipped it were over 4 times likelier to reach impasse (Morris, Nadler et al.). No salary-specific study compares channels.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Call then written recap, or email only; terms always in writing.                                                                                   |

## 2. Myths and dubious statistics

Correct these when the user repeats them, and don't cite them yourself.

| Claim                                                                                      | What's actually known                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "73% of employers expect you to negotiate"                                                 | CareerBuilder (about 2022) measured willingness, not expectation; methodology not retrievable; later restated as "expect"                            |
| "70% of managers expect negotiation"                                                       | Robert Half, Feb 2019, senior managers only (n > 2,800, 28 US cities); dated                                                                         |
| "87% of employers never rescinded", "19% lost the offer", "under 2% rescind", "94% upheld" | Untraceable vendor figures that don't match Hart et al. (2024)                                                                                       |
| "6% of negotiators lose offers" (from Hart et al.)                                         | Misreading: 1.73 of 26.9 offers isn't conditional on negotiating                                                                                     |
| "Offers are never rescinded for negotiating"                                               | Anecdote (Qureshi), not data; rare but not zero                                                                                                      |
| "84% / 85% of people who negotiate get more"                                               | Jobvite 2017 (sample unknown); Fidelity 2022 self-report where "success" means some of the ask, with survivor bias                                   |
| "Women don't ask: 7% vs 57%"                                                               | One Carnegie Mellon cohort, described inconsistently, not clearly peer-reviewed; contradicted by Pew 2023 and Artz et al. 2018                       |
| "Backlash is 5.5× larger for women"                                                        | From a secondary essay; not in the primary papers                                                                                                    |
| "Not negotiating costs $500k–$1M over a career" / "$5k becomes $100k in 10 years"          | Modelled projections under assumed raises, not measured outcomes                                                                                     |
| "First offers explain 25% (or 50%) of outcomes"                                            | Derived from r ≈ .50 in simulations (Orr & Guthrie, 2006; about .37 for experienced negotiators); not field data                                     |
| "Never name a number first"                                                                | Conditional on information; predates posted ranges and history bans                                                                                  |
| "Employers always leave 10–20% room" / "52% offer below their max"                         | CareerBuilder/Harris 2017 vendor survey; dated                                                                                                       |
| "Pay transparency raises (or lowers) pay"                                                  | Depends on type: posting laws +1.3–3.6% (Colorado); coworker transparency about −2%                                                                  |
| "Common stock is worth 10–20% of preferred"                                                | Untraced vendor rule of thumb                                                                                                                        |
| "The FTC banned non-competes"                                                              | The rule was vacated; the FTC dropped its appeals in Sep 2025                                                                                        |
| "The 13th salary is legally required in Germany"                                           | False: contractual or collective. Austria's legal basis is disputed in the sources                                                                   |
| "The UK is banning salary-history questions"                                               | Not in the official consultation summary                                                                                                             |
| "18 states require pay ranges"                                                             | Trackers disagree (13–18 plus DC)                                                                                                                    |
| "80% who accept a counteroffer leave within 6 months"                                      | Untraceable; the best-cited figure is about 50% within 12 months (CEB, methodology unpublished); Robert Half UK's 74% within a year is self-reported |
| "Indian switches get 30–50% hikes" / "67% of IT firms mandate 90-day notice"               | Vendor and candidate surveys, not market data                                                                                                        |
| "The labour codes cut take-home by 3–5%"                                                   | Vendor estimate                                                                                                                                      |
| "Glassdoor ranges are accurate"                                                            | Glassdoor's own study found ranges contained reported pay about 67% of the time, against unverified self-reports                                     |
| "Google moved to front-loaded vesting in 2025"                                             | Crowdsourced only; no dated announcement                                                                                                             |
| "The two-week minimum deadline is a NACE rule"                                             | NACE sets no minimum; the two weeks is a campus convention                                                                                           |
| "Email is better" (McKenzie) / "calls get bigger raises" (Rora)                            | Practitioner opinion on both sides                                                                                                                   |

Disputed dates to state as disputed: Massachusetts' cure period (ends Oct 2026 or Oct 2027), New York's Trapped at Work Act (19 Dec 2026 on most readings; possibly 13 Feb 2027), Malta's transposition status.

## 3. Regional and contested advice

- Whether the EU directive binds private employers before national transposition: practitioners split.
- US federal: OPM's rule and fact sheet say agencies may not consider non-federal salary or a competing offer; at least one agency (GSA, April 2026) says it no longer applies the 2024 changes. Assume a competing offer can't be used unless the agency's HR says otherwise.
- India: whether the new wage definition applies to gratuity retrospectively; whether an employer can withhold a relieving letter; whether the 50% test runs against total remuneration or CTC.
- UAE gratuity: calendar vs working days, and the cap's basis.
- UK Civil Service: how much room exists below the Senior Civil Service.
- Naming the competing company: credibility vs verification and poaching dynamics; the user's call.
- Exploding-offer norms (two weeks, extensions) are US campus norms; elsewhere, ask anyway.
- Using a competing offer raised women's pay but not how they were seen in lab vignettes (Bowles & Babcock 2013), and doesn't work in US federal hiring under OPM's rule.
- Invoking a family decision-maker: acceptable only if true.

## 4. Market data sources and their limits

| Source                                                                                 | Measures                                                         | Limits                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legally posted ranges                                                                  | The employer's good-faith range for this role                    | Pay skews to the lower half; ranges contained reported pay about 67% of the time (Glassdoor); Colorado allows pay outside them                  |
| BLS OEWS                                                                               | US wages by occupation and area, base plus production incentives | Excludes non-production bonuses; equity not included; no levels; lagged                                                                         |
| US DOL OFLC LCA disclosures                                                            | Wage attested on H-1B filings, by employer, title and worksite   | Base only; employer-entered and unverified; many filings never become hires; names unstandardised; lagged                                       |
| Levels.fyi                                                                             | Self-reported total pay with a verified subset (offer letters)   | Tech-heavy and self-selected; "only site that verifies" is its own claim                                                                        |
| Glassdoor                                                                              | Self-reports plus an undisclosed model estimate                  | Unverified, method undisclosed, old entries not aged out; weak                                                                                  |
| MGMA, SullivanCotter, AMGA, AAMC                                                       | Physician pay and production by specialty and region             | Paid; disagree widely (decoding.md §7)                                                                                                          |
| AAUP, CUPA-HR, public records                                                          | Faculty and staff pay                                            | Field and rank lags                                                                                                                             |
| OPM GS tables, civil-service adverts, NHS bands                                        | Statutory scales                                                 | The step or point within the band is what's negotiated                                                                                          |
| Carta equity benchmarks; Index Ventures OptionPlan (European grants by role and stage) | Startup equity grant size by stage and role                      | Self-selected company data; check the date                                                                                                      |
| Entgeltatlas (Germany)                                                                 | Median pay by occupation and region                              | Medians only; no company or level detail                                                                                                        |

## 5. The 2025–26 market

- ZipRecruiter's Q1 2025 new-hire survey: 31% negotiated, down from 49%; sign-on bonuses fell from 43% to 20%; about 28% of hires who saw a posted range landed at or below its bottom.
- Korn Ferry (Oct 2025): "best and final" first offers are spreading beyond tech, "a strong signal rather than a hard stop".
- The US hires rate was 3.1% in February 2026, the lowest since April 2020 (BLS JOLTS).
- So: calibrate asks downward in a soft market, keep them reasoned, and still ask.

## 6. What earlier guides do well and badly

- **Qureshi, "Ten Rules" (2016):** good on getting everything in writing, keeping the door open, giving reasons, pushing back on exploding offers and the "if you can do X, I'll sign" close. Dated "never reveal a number"; anecdotal evidence; sympathetic reasons and invented decision-makers can slide into fabrication.
- **McKenzie, "Salary Negotiation" (2012):** good on negotiating after a "yes-if", trading across issues and moving to manager-controlled items. US-tech and dated: the "$1" form trick and quoting past pay plus 5–10% (now often an unlawful question).
- **Malhotra (HBR, 2014):** the most balanced: likability, understanding constraints, all issues at once, never lying, ignoring ultimatums.
- **Harvard PON, university career centres:** good BATNA framing and decision-time norms; generic on regional law.
- **Paid services (Levels.fyi, Rora):** real data and recruiter experience; self-reported outcomes, selection bias, US big-tech focus.
- **Gap across all of them:** private equity as a range with preference and dilution caveats, 13th/14th-month pay, CTC and gratuity bases, and public-sector scales. This skill covers those.
