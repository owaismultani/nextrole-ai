## 1. Three layers of authority

1. **The employer's instructions win**: the ad, an application form, or a mandated template such as Japan's rirekisho, Indian public-sector biodata or South African government forms.
2. **Law** says what the employer may not demand. It almost never forbids a candidate from volunteering something. Tell the user what is optional and that they may decline; never refuse to format an optional personal field they choose (photo, date of birth, nationality, marital status, pronouns). National ID numbers, bank details, and patient, client or classified identifiers stay must-fix: explain the risk and leave them off unless a mandated form explicitly requires one.
3. **Local convention** is the default when the first two are silent.

## 2. The minimal-data default

Never add any of these on your own: religion, caste, ethnicity or race, health or disability, sexual orientation, political or union membership, criminal record, pregnancy or children, height or weight, national ID numbers (SSN, SIN, NRIC, Aadhaar, PESEL, CPF, BSN, personnummer, CPR, Korean resident number), bank details, or a full street address. Add one only when the user asks and the target norm supports it, and even then say it is optional and name the trade-off.

Photo and date of birth are the two contested fields. Decide them per country with sections 3–4, give the evidence (section 12 and evidence.md), and let the user choose.

## 3. Americas, UK and Ireland, Oceania, Africa

Find the country's heading and read only that block. Legend: **Yes** expected, **Opt** optional or mixed, **No** omit.

### US

- **Document, length:** "Resume"; 1 page early career, 2 experienced; federal max 2 (Sep 2025)
- **Photo:** No (EEOC: employers "should not ask for a photograph")
- **Date of birth:** No (age discrimination law protects 40+)
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt: "Authorized to work in the US" if useful
- **Address:** City, state
- **References:** No; no "available on request"
- **Notes:** Letter paper. New York City added height and weight as protected (2023)

### Canada

- **Document, length:** Resume, up to 2
- **Photo:** No
- **Date of birth:** No (an Ontario tribunal found a DOB field breached the Code)
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt: "Legally entitled to work in Canada" only
- **Address:** City, province
- **References:** No; separate sheet when asked
- **Notes:** Never the SIN. Ontario Human Rights Commission policy treats a "Canadian experience" requirement as discriminatory; count foreign experience fully

### UK

- **Document, length:** "CV", up to 2 A4; 3 for senior; academic longer
- **Photo:** No, except acting and modelling
- **Date of birth:** No
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt: right-to-work or visa line
- **Address:** Town
- **References:** Optional "available on request"
- **Notes:** Name the file after the person, not "CV". Health questions barred pre-offer (Equality Act s.60)

### Ireland

- **Document, length:** CV, 2
- **Photo:** No
- **Date of birth:** No
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt: Stamp or permit line
- **Address:** Town
- **References:** Opt
- **Notes:** Equality grounds include family status and membership of the Traveller community (not verified)

### Australia

- **Document, length:** Resume, 1–3 by experience (Queensland Gov); Seek says 1–2
- **Photo:** No unless asked
- **Date of birth:** No (one university lists it as optional)
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes: state work rights ("Full working rights" or visa subclass)
- **Address:** Suburb, postcode
- **References:** Yes: usually 2 referees, or "on request"
- **Notes:** Ask referees first. Public-sector guides allow up to 4 pages

### New Zealand

- **Document, length:** CV, 2–3
- **Photo:** No
- **Date of birth:** No
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes: work rights
- **Address:** Suburb
- **References:** Yes: 2 referees common
- **Notes:** Human Rights Act 1993 s.23 bars forms that indicate intent to discriminate (not verified)

### Brazil

- **Document, length:** Currículo, 1–2
- **Photo:** Opt (no ban on requesting one, but risky under anti-discrimination law)
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** Opt, trending No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **Address:** City, state
- **References:** Opt
- **Notes:** Labour law bars ads referencing sex, age, colour or family situation. Data-protection law (LGPD) applies before hiring. Omit the CPF

### Mexico

- **Document, length:** Currículum, 1–2
- **Photo:** Opt (still common; State of Mexico bans photo CVs for state and municipal jobs)
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **Address:** City
- **References:** Opt
- **Notes:** Employers may not require non-pregnancy certificates. A June 2026 Senate "blind CV" bill is not law

### South Africa

- **Document, length:** CV, 2–3; government posts up to 3
- **Photo:** No
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes: citizen or permit status
- **Address:** Town
- **References:** Yes: at least 2 for government posts
- **Notes:** Race and gender go on Employment Equity forms, not the CV. Some public adverts ask for current salary notch. Certified copies attached separately

## 4. Europe, Asia and the Gulf

### Germany

- **Document, length:** Lebenslauf, tabular, 1–2; plus cover letter and attachments
- **Photo:** Opt: customary, voluntary under the AGG; tech and international employers increasingly omit it
- **Date of birth:** Opt: customary (DD.MM.YYYY)
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** Yes: employer references (Arbeitszeugnisse) and certificates as copies
- **Signature or clause:** Opt: place, date and signature; still expected at banks, insurers, public sector
- **Notes:** Gapless timeline with labelled gaps. CEFR language levels. Photo, if used: recent, professional, top right

### Austria

- **Document, length:** Lebenslauf, 1–2
- **Photo:** Opt: customary

- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt

- **Signature or clause:** Opt
- **Notes:** Similar to Germany

### Switzerland

- **Document, length:** CV up to 2 (3 for long careers), plus a dossier
- **Photo:** Opt to Yes in German-speaking cantons
- **Date of birth:** Opt: common (ZHAW: optional)
- **Marital:** Opt (sources disagree)
- **Nationality, permit:** Permit (L, B or C) for non-Swiss: Yes; nationality: common, optional

- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** No
- **Signature or clause:** No

### Netherlands

- **Document, length:** CV up to 2 (ICT may be longer), plus motivation letter
- **Photo:** Opt

- **Signature or clause:** No
- **Notes:** Omit the BSN

### Sweden, Norway, Denmark

- **Document, length:** CV up to 2 A4, plus a short personal letter (half to one page, read)
- **Photo:** Opt: "common but optional" (Sweden)
- **Date of birth:** Opt (Denmark more traditional)
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** Opt (Norway: 2 named referees typical)
- **Signature or clause:** No
- **Notes:** Never the personal ID number. Name parental leave rather than leaving a gap

### Spain, Italy, Portugal

- **Document, length:** CV 1–2; Europass common in the public sector
- **Photo:** Opt, declining
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** No
- **Signature or clause:** Italy: the data-processing consent line is legally unnecessary for CVs but still widely expected
- **Notes:** Weakly sourced row

### Poland

- **Document, length:** CV 1–2
- **Photo:** Opt
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** No
- **Signature or clause:** Opt: a GDPR (RODO) clause isn't needed for the standard data in the Labour Code, but practice still expects a short one
- **Notes:** Add a separate clause only if the user wants to stay on file for future recruitment

### India

- **Document, length:** Resume 1–2 (private); "biodata" for government and public-sector units
- **Photo:** Private: No. If the form asks: Yes
- **Date of birth:** Opt (government: Yes)
- **Marital:** Private: No. Public sector and banks: often Yes
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **References, attachments:** Opt
- **Signature or clause:** Declaration ("I hereby declare…" with place, date, signature): omit for product companies and multinationals; include for public sector, government, some IT services
- **Notes:** Father's name, religion and caste only when the notification demands them

### Japan

- **Document, length:** Rirekisho (fixed form) plus shokumu keirekisho (free-form career history, about 1–2 A4, not verified)
- **Photo:** Yes (the form has a photo box)
- **Date of birth:** Yes
- **Marital:** No (spouse and dependents fields removed from the 2021 official template)
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes
- **Notes:** The rirekisho is identification; the career history is the document that gets evaluated. Gender is optional on the 2021 labour-ministry template; the old JIS sample was withdrawn in 2020

### South Korea

- **Document, length:** Resume plus a company-prompted personal essay
- **Photo:** Opt in private sector (still common); No in public-sector blind hiring
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** No (illegal to request)
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt
- **Notes:** Employers with 30+ staff may not request appearance, height, weight, birthplace, marital status, assets or family background (2019). A 2024 private-sector photo-ban bill's status is unknown. Omit the resident number

### China

- **Document, length:** 1 page, often a platform template
- **Photo:** Yes, common
- **Date of birth:** Yes, common
- **Marital:** Employers may not ask women about marriage or childbearing (2023 law, not verified)
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes
- **Notes:** Age caps ("35") are a widespread grey area; follow the platform's fields

### Singapore

- **Document, length:** Resume, 2
- **Photo:** No (photo requested at offer stage)
- **Date of birth:** No
- **Marital:** No
- **Nationality, permit:** Opt: work pass status is useful
- **Notes:** Fair-employment guidelines say to remove age, gender, race, religion, marital or family status, disability, photo and NRIC. The Workplace Fairness Act is passed, with commencement targeted for end-2027

### Malaysia

- **Document, length:** Resume 2–3
- **Photo:** Opt, common
- **Date of birth:** Opt
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Yes
- **References, attachments:** Opt
- **Notes:** Unsourced; follow the form

### UAE, Saudi Arabia, Gulf

- **Document, length:** CV 2–3
- **Photo:** Opt to common (contested). Default: include for local employers and regional job boards if the user opts in; omit for multinationals
- **Date of birth:** Opt, common; include only on opt-in
- **Marital:** Opt
- **Nationality, permit:** Expected by recruiters for visa and local-hiring-quota (Emiratisation, Saudisation) decisions, but the user decides: nationality and visa status ("UAE resident, employment visa" or "Overseas, available on 30 days' notice")
- **References, attachments:** Opt
- **Signature or clause:** No
- **Notes:** Practitioner sources only. Some UAE recruiters advise dropping the photo. Offer notice period and driving licence if held

## 5. Europass

A voluntary EU format in 31 languages; its own guide still suggests a photo. The only recruiter-perception data is old and small (70 Hungarian HR staff in 2015 valued the structure and saw it as a format for career starters). Use Europass when the employer, an EU institution or a public tender asks for it. Otherwise use a national-style CV and keep only the CEFR language grid, which readers across Europe understand.

## 7. Data protection and consent clauses

- **EU, EEA and UK (GDPR):** data minimisation applies; employers should collect only what the role needs. Health, ethnicity, religion and similar are special-category data, so leave them off. Candidates have rights of access and erasure. The UK regulator's recruitment guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- **Consent clauses** matter only by custom: Poland and Italy (section 4). Brazil (LGPD), South Africa (POPIA), China (PIPL) and Singapore (PDPA) follow the same minimisation logic without a CV clause.
- In October 2025 Italy's data-protection authority fined a school €3,000 for circulating candidate CVs to families; consent was not a valid basis. Useful when a user asks why the clause doesn't protect them.

## 8. Work authorisation

- Work authorisation is not nationality. Prefer a neutral line: "Authorised to work in the UK; no sponsorship required", "Swiss B permit", "Full working rights (Australia)".
- State citizenship only where it's the norm (Germany, Austria and Switzerland traditionally; the Gulf; Chinese and Japanese forms) or the role requires it (some defence jobs).
- If the user needs sponsorship, they choose whether to say so on the CV or in the application form. Never misstate status.

## 9. Language, paper size and dates

- Write in the language of the ad (Swedish and Swiss guidance say so explicitly).
- US Letter for the US and Canada; A4 everywhere else.
- Unambiguous month-and-year dates ("Mar 2022"). DD.MM.YYYY is expected for personal data in German-speaking countries.
- Phone numbers in international format for cross-border applications.

## 10. Conversion recipes

Make every conversion on a copy, never on the master (see patches.md). Recipe lines without a tag are `convention`.

**Any conversion**, as a checklist:

1. Personal details: walk the target country's block in sections 3–4 field by field (photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality or permit, address, references, signature or clause).
2. Paper size, page locale and date format (section 9).
3. Length for the target market and the user's career stage.
4. Attachments: references, certificates or a dossier only where the target expects them.
5. Language of the ad; translated section headings.
6. A work-authorisation line where it removes doubt (section 8).
7. Anything the source country expects that the target finds odd (a hobbies block, a declaration, a photo) goes, on the copy.

**Germany → US** (also the answer to "I'm moving from Germany to the US, what should I change?"):

0. Confirm the target, or state the assumption: "Assumed: private-sector role. Academic and federal applications differ; tell me if that's you." An academic target keeps a CV; a federal target follows industries.md, section 13.
1. Translate the content into US English. Keep the meaning exact and never upgrade a title in translation. For terms without a direct equivalent, give the English plus the original: "Working Student (Werkstudent), 20 h/week"; "Apprenticeship, Industrial Clerk (Ausbildung zum Industriekaufmann; dual vocational training)"; Praktikum → Internship. Drop the Abitur once there is a degree.
2. For employers US readers won't know, add a short descriptor from the user's facts: "Müller GmbH (industrial pumps, ~800 employees)".
3. Add a 2–4 line summary for experienced candidates, and call the document a "resume"; in the US a "CV" usually means an academic CV.
4. Remove the photo, date of birth, place of birth, nationality, marital status and religion; drop the "Persönliche Daten" block.
5. Full street address → city and state (or "Relocating to Austin, TX", only if true).
6. Remove place, date and signature at the end, and the references section.
7. Turn the tabular timeline into achievement bullets under each role (hand to resume-bullet-writer if available). Keep labelled gaps.
8. Keep the original degree name and grade with its scale; don't convert to a US GPA. Add a plain-English description of the field.
9. Languages: plain words plus CEFR ("German (native), English (C1)").
10. Drop the hobbies section unless relevant; don't attach Arbeitszeugnisse unless asked.
11. Add a work-authorisation line only if it helps; ask whether the user needs sponsorship. Never state an authorisation the user doesn't hold.
12. Length 1–2 pages, US Letter, `en-US` locale.
13. With MCP: `duplicate_resume` as "<Name> — US", apply patches.md section 9, then hand bullets to resume-bullet-writer if available.

**US → Germany (traditional employer):**

- Before: `Jane Doe · Austin, TX · jane@example.com`
- After: `Jane Doe · [street], [postcode] München · +49 … · geb. [DD.MM.YYYY] · Staatsangehörigkeit: US; Aufenthaltstitel: [permit]`
- Add a photo only if the user opts in. Add place, date and signature only for paper applications or banks, insurers and the public sector. Attach references and certificates. Tech and international employers: keep it closer to the US version.

**India (biodata) → UK:**

- Before: `Father's name: …; DOB: …; Marital status: Married; Religion: …; Declaration: I hereby declare…`
- After: `Name · Manchester · +44 … · email · LinkedIn · Right to work: Skilled Worker visa (sponsorship required)`. Remove the rest.

**Any country → Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia)** (`convention`, practitioner sources):

- Offer nationality and visa status (Gulf recruiters expect them for visa and local-hiring-quota decisions; the user decides), plus notice period and driving licence if held.
- Photo and date of birth: optional to common, and contested. Include them only if the user opts in; suggest including for regional job boards and local employers, and omitting on global applicant-tracking forms and multinationals (`convention`, practitioner sources only).
- Length 2–3 pages is normal.

## 11. AI in hiring: candidate rights as of October 2026

Explain these only when the user asks. Never claim an employer uses such a tool unless its own notice says so. Verify before relying on any of them.

| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

Draft notice rules withdrawn June 2026                                                         | Expect notice; use city and state rather than a full address                                                                                                                  |
| California FEHA regulations | Effective 1 October 2025                                                                                                                                                                     | Ask for accommodation or an alternative assessment, for example for video or game-based tests                                                                                 |

## 13. Changed in 2025–2026

Re-check these before relying on them:

- US federal 2-page resume cap from 27 September 2025.
- Colorado SB 26-189 replaced SB 24-205, effective 1 January 2027.
- Illinois HB 3773 live since 1 January 2026; its notice rules were withdrawn in June 2026.
- California FEHA automated-decision regulations from 1 October 2025.
- EU Digital Omnibus moved employment AI duties to 2 December 2027.
- UK regulator's recruitment guidance under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- Singapore Workplace Fairness Act commencement targeted for end-2027.
- China raised civil-service age caps in 2025 and issued an April 2026 policy against hukou and age restrictions (policy, not statute).
- Mexico's June 2026 blind-CV bill is pending; a 2024 Korean private-sector photo-ban bill is pending.
