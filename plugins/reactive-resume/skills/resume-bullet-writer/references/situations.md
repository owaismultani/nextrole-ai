## 1. Roles with few obvious numbers

Care, teaching, creative, admin, trades and many public-sector jobs have results, just not on a dashboard. Use these levers, and accept that some bullets will carry no number:

| Lever                     | Prompt                                                             | Pattern                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scale or scope            | How many people, accounts, sites, beds, students, lines, products? | "…for [N] clinics", "…across a [N]-bed unit"                |
| Frequency or volume       | How many per shift, day, week or term?                             | "Reviewed [N]–[N] articles a week"                          |
| Time                      | What got faster? Time saved per cycle?                             | "cut [process] from [N] to [N] days"                        |
| Quality or errors         | Errors, rework, complaints, audit or inspection results?           | "[N] findings in [N] external audits"                       |
| Money                     | Budget held, savings, cost avoided?                                | "managed a [amount] budget"                                 |
| Rank or selection         | Ranked among peers? Picked from how many?                          | "1 of [N] selected for…"                                    |
| Target attainment         | Percent of goal, quota or service level?                           | "[N]% of [target]"                                          |
| Before and after          | What was the state before, and after?                              | "from paper logs to a shared tracker used by all [N] staff" |
| Adoption or reach         | Who uses it now? How many teams, downloads, attendees?             | "adopted by [N] of [N] teams"                               |
| Retention or satisfaction | Staff or customer retention, survey scores?                        | "kept all [N] team members year over year"                  |
| Recognition               | Awards, promotion, being asked to train others?                    | "chosen to train [N] new hires"                             |
| Speed of ramp             | Time to promotion or to working independently?                     | "promoted within [N] months"                                |

Range and frequency phrasing ("[N]–[N] a week") works well for roles that never had a counter. For care roles, competencies with named equipment, patient populations and certifications are concrete scope and read as strong bullets.

## 2. Confidential figures and NDAs

Ask first: "Is this figure public, or covered by an NDA?" Then prefer, in this order:

1. Percentages or ratios without absolute amounts ("grew segment revenue [N]%").
2. Ranges or orders of magnitude ("seven-figure budget", "[N],000+ users").
3. Relative terms with a referent ("highest close rate on a [N]-person team").
4. Scope without the outcome.

Anonymise clients by category only when the category doesn't identify them. A well-known case: a candidate barred from naming a client wrote "a large software company in Redmond, Washington", which identified the client anyway and was read as a breach. Take extra care with unreleased financials of a listed company. (This ordering is practitioner judgement with thin sourcing; use it as a default, not a rule.)

## 3. Professions with confidentiality duties

| Profession                            | Rule of thumb for bullets                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Law                                   | Name clients or matters only with client consent. Present past results with context (their role, the matter type) rather than bare verdict amounts; some bars (e.g. North Carolina) have held that a disclaimer doesn't cure a misleading results claim. Rules vary by jurisdiction as of 2026; the user should check their bar's guidance. |
| Healthcare                            | Nothing that could identify a patient: no names, dates of care, rare conditions tied to a place. Unit-level quality data takes "contributed to" wording.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Education                             | No student names or identifiable details; report class or cohort results, not individuals.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Cleared or classified government work | Describe only what has been cleared for public release; use unclassified program descriptions and scope. Tell the user to follow their agency's pre-publication guidance.                                                                                                                                                                   |

## 4. Team versus individual credit

- Ask what the user personally did whenever they say "we". People under-claim at least as often as they over-claim.
- Choose the verb from the ownership table in verb-bank.md.
- The strongest honest pattern is the team result plus the user's own part and, where they have one, their own metric: "As a member of the [N]-person team that grew [X] [N]%, personally reached [N]% of target."
- Unit-, site- or company-level metrics need scope wording ("on a unit that…", "team of [N] that…").

## 5. Calibrating to seniority

Practitioner synthesis; no controlled study compares these.

| Level                 | Lead with                                                         | Typical metrics                                                 | First bullet or scope line                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Entry or student      | Concrete tasks done well, selection, recognition                  | Volume, accuracy, ranking, target attainment                    | The environment ("a [N]-seat restaurant")   |
| Mid-level             | Things built, fixed or shipped, and their effect                  | Time, quality, cost, users                                      | The product, system or segment owned        |
| Senior or staff       | Decisions, designs and standards others adopted; cross-team reach | Adoption, reliability, cost at organisation scale, risk avoided | Systems and teams influenced                |
| Manager               | Team results plus how they were built (hiring, coaching, process) | Team KPIs, retention, hiring, delivery predictability           | Headcount, budget, remit                    |
| Director or executive | Strategy, P&L and organisation-level results; few, dense bullets  | Revenue, margin, growth, market position, org size              | P&L, headcount, geographies, reporting line |

Managers and executives shouldn't list individual tasks unless the target job needs them. Individual contributors shouldn't borrow executive verbs ("set strategy") without the scope to back them.

## 6. Bullet count, length and tense

- **Count:** 3–5 bullets for the most recent or relevant roles (up to 6 when the role is the main evidence for the target job), 1–3 for older roles, or just the title for very old ones. This is a heuristic, not research.
- **Length:** 1–2 lines, roughly 12–28 words, one idea per bullet. Long sentences scored poorly in recruiter eye-tracking work.
- **Tense:** past for finished roles and completed wins; present for ongoing scope in the current role. Sources split on completed wins in the current role; pick one convention and keep it consistent within the role.
- **Order:** most relevant bullet first. A scope line goes first only when it adds information the title doesn't.

## 7. Students and new graduates

- Part-time and service jobs count. Specific detail is valued even for modest work: volume, accuracy, reliability, being trusted with keys or cash, training others.
- Class, capstone and personal projects take the same treatment: what was built, for whom, with what result (users, grade, prize, adoption).
- Clubs and volunteering: scale (members, funds raised, events) and what changed.
- Employers hiring graduates say they want examples of teamwork, problem-solving and communication rather than lists of those skills, so make each bullet evidence of one.

## 8. Career changers, freelancers and veterans

- **Career changers:** order bullets by relevance to the target role and describe results in terms the new field recognises. Don't relabel past work with the new field's titles. Deciding what to keep is resume-content-guide's job.
- **Freelancers and contractors:** count clients, repeat clients, projects and referrals; name client types rather than clients unless the user has permission.
- **Veterans:** translate rank and unit jargon into scope a civilian reads (people led, equipment value, budget, mission outcome). Keep selection facts; they translate well: "One of [N] officers selected to redesign [program] for [N] personnel". Add "lead" only if the user says they led it.

## 9. Special formats and regions

| Format or region                   | What changes for bullets                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Academic CV                        | List outputs (publications, grants, teaching, supervision) in their conventional formats rather than impact bullets. Use bullets only on an industry-facing resume.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| US federal (Title 5)               | As of 2026, OPM's Merit Hiring Plan caps resumes at 2 pages (from 27 September 2025); longer ones make the applicant ineligible. Per role, include job title and employer (plus series and grade for any federal position), start and end dates as MM/YYYY, and hours worked per week, in concise, results-focused language. Old "write a long federal resume" advice is outdated. Verify current OPM guidance before relying on it. |
| UK                                 | About 2–3 lines per job, often structured as STAR. The official National Careers Service guide says nothing about numbers, so quantify where honest without forcing it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Japan (職務経歴書, career history) | Express results with numbers and objective indicators an outsider understands, don't overstate, keep a consistent polite register; usually 1–2 A4 pages.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Germany and DACH                   | Practitioner consensus (not verified against an official source) favours a factual tone over sales language. Keep the numbers and cut the hype.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Other regions                      | Defer to resume-content-guide for country norms.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

## 10. Writing for AI screeners

A 2025–26 simulation study found that language models rank resumes written by the same model higher. The pipelines were simulated, the headline numbers changed between versions, and nothing shows the effect in real hiring. Don't trade truth or human readability for "AI-friendly" phrasing, and never add hidden text or instructions aimed at a screener.
